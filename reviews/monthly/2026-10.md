## Executive Summary

The supplied October 2026 papers point to progress in AI-enabled photovoltaics across model evaluation, materials representation, scientific literature analysis, and power-system operation. The clearest methodological advance is a stronger emphasis on credible generalization: provenance-grouped evaluation exposes duplication-driven leakage in perovskite datasets, while quantitative retrieval benchmarks test scientific assistants against measured device records. Physics-aware representations, graph neural networks, and machine-learned atomistic simulations aim to connect predictions more closely to chemistry and device physics. At the deployment level, multimodal forecasting and LLM-driven energy-system optimization broaden AI's role beyond individual solar cells. These developments should not be read as uniformly demonstrated performance breakthroughs: several abstracts are truncated, many omit quantitative comparisons, and several supplied category labels describe papers without an explicit AI contribution.

## Major Themes

- Evaluation beyond random splits: grouping devices by recipe, publication, and institution provides more demanding tests of generalization than conventional random partitions.
- Grounded scientific assistants: quantitative retrieval benchmarks and verified-record workflows shift attention from fluent answers toward traceable experimental evidence.
- Chemistry-aware prediction: virtual-alloy representations and heterogeneous graph learning seek to represent compositions, interfaces, and device architectures more meaningfully.
- AI-accelerated materials physics: equivariant neural networks and machine-learned interatomic potentials extend access to optical responses, lattice dynamics, and defect behavior.
- Multiscale PV forecasting: transformers, graph models, multimodal fusion, and shared pretraining address temporal variation, spatial cloud dynamics, and transfer across sites.
- Testing the value of physical constraints: large-scale evaluations distinguish plausible physics-informed architectures from demonstrated improvements in accuracy or transferability.
- Agents and operational control: LLM-based design search and reinforcement-learning dispatch connect PV with storage, buildings, charging infrastructure, and grid constraints.
- Experimental foundations for future AI: interface chemistry and nondestructive degradation measurements supply mechanistic information, although these studies do not themselves establish AI advances.

## Field Progress

- Photovoltaic ML evaluation is becoming more realistic: provenance-aware splits expose recipe recall and institutional overlap that can masquerade as predictive generalization.
- Scientific literature assistance is gaining measurable targets through record-derived numerical answers and explicit evidence-verification workflows.
- Materials modeling is moving beyond categorical composition labels toward representations of alloy continuity, molecular symmetry, heterogeneous substructures, and device interfaces.
- PV forecasting research increasingly combines spatial and temporal information with uncertainty calibration and cross-site reuse, addressing operational needs beyond point accuracy.
- AI applications span a wider photovoltaic workflow, from optical-property surrogates and lattice simulations to energy-system design and dispatch; evidence of laboratory-validated AI-led solar-cell improvement remains limited in the supplied excerpts.

## Open Questions

- Which models retain useful accuracy when duplicate recipes, publications, institutions, and future devices are all separated from training data?
- Can physics-aware alloy and interface representations extrapolate to genuinely new chemistries, and how should uncertainty be validated in those regimes?
- Can retrieval and multi-agent systems reliably reconcile inconsistent measurements, preserve provenance, and abstain when experimental evidence is incomplete?
- Under what data volumes, climates, forecasting horizons, and distribution shifts does physics-informed learning outperform well-tuned data-driven baselines?
- Do forecasting pretraining, calibrated uncertainty, and agent-guided optimization deliver measurable operational savings without compromising grid safety?
- How can nondestructive degradation measurements and interface-chemistry findings become standardized training data for lifetime prediction and experimentally verified device optimization?

## Outlook

The next useful step is to connect these methodological advances through shared, leakage-resistant benchmarks and prospective experimental or operational tests. For solar-cell research, that means evaluating chemistry-aware models on genuinely unseen compositions and linking predictions to fabrication, efficiency, and stability measurements. For PV operations, it means testing transferable forecasts and decision-making agents under changing weather, hardware, and network constraints. Progress should be judged by reproducible generalization, calibrated uncertainty, and measured downstream benefit—not by architectural complexity alone.

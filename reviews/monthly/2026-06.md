## Executive Summary

June 2026 showed a clear shift in AI for solar-cell research from isolated model benchmarking toward tightly coupled, physics-aware workflows that accelerate both discovery and reliability analysis. Across perovskites, organic photovoltaics, and device engineering, AI was used to screen large compositional spaces, infer degradation mechanisms, forecast stability time series, optimize energy-management decisions, and build more expressive surrogate models for optical and electronic properties. Several papers paired machine learning with first-principles or drift-diffusion simulations, indicating that the field is moving from purely predictive ML toward hybrid methods that are interpretable, uncertainty-aware, and usable for design decisions under realistic constraints.

## Major Themes

- Physics-informed ML is increasingly paired with first-principles simulation for property prediction, screening, and inverse design.
- Perovskite stability and degradation remain a central target, with Bayesian inference, time-series forecasting, and imaging-based diagnostics gaining traction.
- Foundation models and transfer learning are being adapted to cold-start PV forecasting and sparse-data materials problems.
- Surrogate modeling is improving for spectra, band gaps, and excitonic properties, especially with equivariant or multi-fidelity architectures.
- Device-level optimization is broadening beyond absorber design to include contacts, interfaces, thermal transport, inverter logistics, and system economics.
- Uncertainty quantification and explainability are becoming more common, reflecting a push toward deployable and trustworthy AI tools.
- High-throughput datasets and combinatorial experiments continue to expand the training and validation base for solar-relevant materials discovery.

## Field Progress

- AI is now being used not just to predict photovoltaic properties, but to infer physically meaningful parameters from experiments and simulations.
- Data volume and diversity improved through high-throughput synthesis, large spectral datasets, and multi-site PV forecasting benchmarks.
- Model architectures are becoming more specialized: foundation models for cold-start forecasting, equivariant GNNs for spectra, and physics-informed sequence models for stability.
- Explainability and uncertainty quantification are moving from optional add-ons to core components of solar-materials AI workflows.
- The scope of AI in solar energy has expanded from device efficiency toward durability, manufacturability, maintenance logistics, and grid-level operation.

## Open Questions

- How robust are physics-informed and foundation-model approaches when transferred across radically different chemistries, device architectures, or climates?
- Can AI models infer causal degradation mechanisms rather than only correlate signals with failure modes?
- What standardized benchmarks are needed for comparing uncertainty-aware models in solar materials discovery and PV operation?
- How can surrogate models be coupled with active learning to reduce experimental burden while preserving synthesis realism?
- What is the best way to integrate system-level constraints, such as cost, carbon intensity, and degradation, into AI-guided PV optimization?

## Outlook

The June 2026 literature suggests that AI in solar cells is maturing into a practical enabling layer for the full lifecycle of photovoltaic technologies, from materials discovery to operation and end-user deployment. The most impactful next steps will likely come from hybrid pipelines that combine mechanistic modeling, high-quality experiments, and uncertainty-aware learning. If these approaches continue to scale, the field should see faster translation from candidate materials to stable, manufacturable, and economically viable solar technologies.

PerovAgent: Autonomous Ligand Design for Halide Perovskite Nanocrystals

Seungjun Cha, Yoo Jae Jeong, Amanda Conde Del Moral, Juan-Pablo Correa-Baena, Seth R. Marder, David S. Ginger

Designing surface-capping ligands for high-performance halide perovskite nanocrystals requires navigating a vast molecular space while discovering design rules that govern ligand-surface interactions. Here, we introduce PerovAgent, an autonomous multi-agent framework that combines large language model (LLM)-driven scientific reasoning with computational tools for atomistic model construction, machine-learned interatomic potential evaluation, and statistical validation. Across ten independent campaigns on CsPbBr3 surfaces, PerovAgent adaptively explored and refined diverse ligand chemistries while progressively improving the binding-energy objective. Beyond optimization, the framework converts recurring chemical observations into explicit design principles that can be statistically evaluated. Of 35 formulated principles, 19 were prospectively supported, of which seven also passed the confound audit, highlighting the need to systematically validate plausible LLM-generated hypotheses during autonomous discovery. A recurrent alkynyl-phosphonate principle was further examined using density functional theory, which reproduced the predicted energetic preference. These results demonstrate an autonomous discovery framework that not only searches molecular space and optimizes the scalar objective but also transforms its search trajectory into systematically testable design principles. The framework can also be extended to more complex ligands, including zwitterionic, multidentate, and polymerizable ligands, to explore a wider range of ligand-surface interactions and uncover new design principles for halide perovskite nanocrystals.

Evidence-grounded large language models guide perovskite solar cell design and optimization

YanKai Chen, Zhi Wan, ZiHao Li, Tao Jing

Using large language models to interpret experimental evidence, design discriminating experiments and formulate well-supported mechanistic explanations remains a central challenge in materials research. Here we establish PSM-Bench, an 800-question benchmark developed by perovskite researchers to evaluate scientific reasoning in perovskite photovoltaics. We also introduce OpenPerov, an expert-informed language-model framework that learns from expert-calibrated evidence records reconstructed from 4,035 training articles; these records link experimental strategies, controls and observations to mechanistic interpretations. Structured-evidence adaptation outperformed conventional question-answer training, and OpenPerov Flash ranked first among 13 closed-book systems. OpenPerov Pro raised Scientific Mastery further, from 87.6 to 89.1, by revising selected passages with retrieved evidence. On questions derived from subsequent studies, both models also outscored expert-authored answers. In an experimental test of self-assembled monolayer design, OpenPerov selected a K analogue that outperformed the Na analogue preferred by experts. Devices incorporating the selected contact reached a power conversion efficiency of 27.05% and retained 96.74% of their initial efficiency after 1,000 h of dark thermal ageing at 85 $^\circ$C. These results show that reconstructed scientific reasoning provides an effective basis for building domain models that interpret experiments and inform materials design.

Enrichment-Oriented Virtual Screening of Perovskite Solar Cells through Reinforcement Learning

Yezeyuan Zheng, Ziyang Hu, Maojun Sun, Ruijian Han, Jian Huang, Guanhua Chen

Experimental optimization of perovskite solar cells is constrained by combinatorial diversity in absorber compositions, device layers, and processing, which makes virtual screening attractive for prioritizing recipes before fabrication. We develop a surrogate-guided reinforcement-learning framework that casts recipe generation as virtual screening, enriching candidate shortlists near high-performing experimental neighbors. A frozen Tabular Prior-data Fitted Network (TabPFN) guides recipe generation, while a reference pool of 890 measured devices enables neighborhood-based assessment. Five-fold evaluation on 1,846 devices shows top-ten selection raises mean power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 19.68\% to 23.74\%. Three learned policies are compared with six baselines under 3,000 surrogate evaluations per run. In top-100 shortlists, Proximal policy optimization (PPO) and recurrent PPO (RPPO) achieve neighborhood-supported hit rates of 23\% and 24\% (7.9- and 8.3-fold Pure Random's 2.9\%), versus 10\% (3.4-fold) for the baselines with the highest supported hit rate (L-SHADE and Ax BO, tied) at $K = 100$. Gains coexist with weak-support fractions. Of 9,000 recipes, 65.4\% match a composition family at similarity 0.80, falling to 11.7\% at 0.90. Results support enrichment-oriented prioritization but not experimental performance, seed robustness, or matched-budget advantage.

Hierarchical interfacial anchoring for durable perovskite solar cells

Jiaxin Wu, Hongzhuo Wu, Zuhong Zhang, Xiaoyu Guan, Jinbo Zhao, Yuting Guo

Although inverted perovskite solar cells now deliver certified efficiencies approaching their single-junction limits, their practical deployment remains constrained by the thermo-mechanical fragility of buried interfaces under realistic temperature fluctuations. Here, we identify thermo-mechanically driven, stress-accumulation-dominated adhesion failure at self-assembled monolayer-based buried interfaces as a critical failure mode in inverted perovskite solar cells. To address this issue, we propose an interfacial robustness design strategy based on a homometallic halide pretreatment, which constructs a chemically compatible and highly anchorable interfacial environment on transparent conductive oxides to guide the ordered assembly of SAMs. This interfacial treatment increases the density of surface hydroxyl species and enables hierarchical multipoint interfacial anchoring dominated by metal-oxygen coordination, with Cl- induced hydrogen-bonding interactions acting cooperatively, thereby enhancing anchoring robustness and improving tolerance to thermo-mechanical stress accumulation. With this interfacial strategy, the devices achieve a power conversion efficiency of 27.3% (26.9% certified) and retain 94% of their initial efficiency after 400 temperature cycles between -40 and 85 degrees C (ISOS-T-3). These results demonstrate that rational regulation of buried interfacial anchoring architectures can simultaneously enhance device efficiency and long-term operational reliability, offering a viable pathway toward high-efficiency and durable perovskite photovoltaics.

Ying Tang, Zuhong Zhang, Hongzhuo Wu, Zhihao Li, Zhenhuang Su, Yonggang Yang

Tin-based perovskites are promising candidates for lead-free photovoltaics but suffer from labile Sn$^{2+}$ coordination, rapid crystallization, and severe interfacial defects. Here, we develop a dynamic molecular transformation using 1-acetylpiperazine (PX) to regulate crystallization and interface chemistry simultaneously. During nucleation and growth, the carbonyl group coordinates with Sn$^{2+}$ to stabilize precursor complexes, moderate supersaturation, and enable controlled crystal growth. Upon annealing, PX undergoes in situ molecular transformation, suggested to form amidinium-functionalized species that preferentially occupy surface A-site vacancies, forming a passivation layer and suppressing nonradiative recombination. This coupled bulk-to-interface evolution yields compact, highly crystalline films with improved optoelectronic quality. Devices achieve a power conversion efficiency of 15.18% and retain 94.1% of initial efficiency after 500 h of continuous illumination. This work demonstrates a dynamic molecular engineering approach integrating crystallization control and interface passivation, providing a pathway toward efficient and stable lead-free perovskite photovoltaics.

Strain-Induced Tetragonal-to-Cubic Transition in MAPbI$_3$ from Machine-Learned Molecular Dynamics

Daan Holleman, Luuk Klein Nagelvoort, Monica Morales-Masis, Linn Leppert

Metal-halide perovskites are promising photovoltaic absorbers whose optoelectronic properties are highly tunable. Epitaxial strain offers a route to stabilize desirable phases, tune band gaps, and reduce energetic disorder, but rational strain engineering requires predictive models. Static, zero-temperature "strained-bulk" density functional theory (DFT) calculations, successful for oxide perovskites, neglect the dynamic disorder and anharmonicity that govern halide perovskites at ambient temperature. Here, we show that molecular dynamics (MD) based on machine-learned force fields trained on-the-fly on DFT reproduces the structural and optoelectronic response of methylammonium (MA) lead iodide (MAPbI$_3$) to epitaxial strain at 300 K. We simulate bulk and free-standing slab models across experimentally accessible biaxial strains by locking the in-plane lattice vectors in nanosecond-long simulations. We predict a tetragonal-to-cubic transition at $\sim$0.5% tensile strain, marked by $c/a = 1$ and the loss of the $a^0a^0c^-$ tilting pattern, in agreement with experiments on epitaxial MAPbI$_3$ films on KCl. MA cations retain preferential orientations in the strain-induced cubic phase, unlike in its temperature-induced counterpart. Bulk and slab models yield consistent trends, with surface effects decaying within a few octahedral layers. Band gaps computed as ensemble averages over MD snapshots blue-shift under tensile strain, consistent with experiment, whereas static calculations on averaged structures predict a red shift. Our study positions MD with constrained lattice vectors as a method for predicting finite-temperature strain phase diagrams and guiding the epitaxial design of perovskite absorbers.

Persistent Neutron Irradiation Effects and Partial Recovery in Thin-Film CdS/CdTe Solar Cells

Ashraful Mamun, Andrew Allison, Meng-Jen, Wang, Etee Kawna Roy, Edward Cazalas

Thin-film CdTe solar cells are promising candidates for lightweight space power, but device-level studies of their response to neutron irradiation remain scarce. Here, we report the device performance of CdS/CdTe solar cells irradiated for 10 to 150 min in the thermal irradiator of a TRIGA reactor (90 kW), corresponding to nominal total neutron fluences of 3.3 x 10^14 to 5.0 x 10^15 n cm-2. The cells were characterized after 18 months of post-irradiation storage, once the residual activity has decayed to background levels. Co-irradiated FTO-coated glass reference substrates exhibit a wavelength-dependent transmission loss, with the transmittance at 500 nm decreasing from 80 % to 50 % after 60 min of irradiation. The irradiated cells retain rectifying behavior, but the reverse-bias dark current, photocurrent, and efficiency generally decrease with exposure time. Post-irradiation annealing at 70 °C under one-sun illumination initially improves Jsc and Voc, with the highest efficiency after 4 h. Prolonged annealing, however, causes substantial FF degradation despite continued photocurrent recovery. These trends suggest that irradiation-induced changes in the glass substrate, CdTe absorber, and contacts respond differently to annealing. Understanding underlying defect chemistry and correlating it with device behavior are necessary steps toward radiation-tolerant CdTe photovoltaics for space applications.

Amorphous selenium-alloyed TeOx thin-film solar cells

Zhaoheng Ling, Linqu Luo, Chrysa Aivalioti, Temur Maksudov, Mengxi Zhang, Theodoulos Kalogerakis

Tellurium dioxide (TeO$_2$) is a wide-bandgap oxide with attractive processing versatility and emerging p-type semiconducting behaviour, making it a promising material system for optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications. However, TeO$_2$-derived thin films prepared by scalable manufacturing processes, such as vacuum sublimation, can contain residual metallic Te$^0$ species that introduce trap states, limit hole transport, and promote carrier recombination. Here, we demonstrate a selenium alloying strategy to control the defect chemistry of vacuum-sublimed TeO$_x$ films. TeO$_2$:Se powder mixtures (90:10~wt\%) were thermally evaporated and subsequently annealed in air at 225~$^\circ$C for up to 9~h, yielding amorphous TeO$_x$:Se films with an optical bandgap of 0.98~eV under optimised conditions. Structural, chemical, optical, and electrical analyses show that prolonged annealing promotes Se redistribution, suppresses residual Te$^0$ to approximately 0.4\%, reduces the film's surface roughness, and shifts the valence-band edge. To evaluate the potential of TeO$_x$:Se for photovoltaic applications, we fabricated thin-film solar cells with an ITO/ZnO/TeO$_x$:Se/MoO$_x$/Ag architecture. Devices based on an 80~nm TeO$_x$:Se active layer annealed for 9~h exhibit optimal performance, with broadband photocurrent generation from 300 to 1000~nm, yielding an open-circuit voltage of 0.50~V, a short-circuit current density of 8.50~mA~cm$^{-2}$, a fill factor of 0.44, and a maximum power conversion efficiency of approximately 1.87\%. These results establish selenium-alloyed TeO$_x$ as a viable oxide-derived absorber for thin-film photovoltaics and highlight chemical-state control as an effective route for improving photovoltaic performance.

Effects of Yttrium doping on Proton Conductivity in Ba(Fe, Co, Zr, Y)O$_{3-δ}$ Cathode Materials for Proton Ceramic Fuel Cells

Chiyoung Kim, Ryan Jacobs, Jun Meng, Jack H. Duffy, Kyle S. Brinkman, Yueh-Lin Lee

Protonic ceramic fuel cells (PCFCs) offer efficient energy conversion at intermediate temperatures, but their performance is often constrained by sluggish oxygen reduction kinetics at the cathode. Triple conducting perovskite materials such as BaCo$_x$Fe$_y$Zr$_z$Y$_{1-x-y-z}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFZY) have shown promise as air electrodes. In this study, we investigated the impact of Y doping on proton conductivity by decoupling the effects on proton diffusivity and proton concentration. Using ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations, we assessed proton tracer diffusivity across three compositions: BaCo$_{0.4}$Fe$_{0.4}$Zr$_{0.2}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFZ), BaCo$_{0.4}$Fe$_{0.4}$Zr$_{0.1}$Y$_{0.1}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFZY0.1) and BaCo$_{0.4}$Fe$_{0.4}$Y$_{0.2}$O$_{3-δ}$ (BCFY). Our results suggest that increasing the Y content from BCFZ to BCFY increases proton diffusivity by 1.7% at 500 $^\circ$C. Under PCFC operating gas conditions, proton concentration and proton conductivity are predicted to increase by 3.8% and 5.3%, respectively, at 500 $^\circ$C. These increases are modest compared with the larger enhancements reported experimentally under reducing conditions, highlighting the importance of gas conditions in evaluating the effects of Y doping. Under low oxygen partial pressure conditions, we predict that the dominant proton transport mechanism shifts from hydroxyl groups (OH$_O^{\bullet}$) to hydride species (H$_O^{\bullet}$). This work provides mechanistic insight into proton transport in BCFZY cathodes and offers design principles for optimizing proton-conducting air electrodes in next-generation PCFCs.

DeepDOSReasoner: Physics-Grounded Reasoning for Density-of-States Prediction and Materials Discovery

Yingheng Wang, Francesco Ricci, Zhilong Wang, Tao Yu, Junwen Bai, Xiangyun Lei

Predicting a material's behavior from its atomic structure is a longstanding goal of the physical sciences. The electronic and phonon densities of states (eDOS and phDOS) govern key electronic, optical, vibrational, and thermodynamic properties, yet their calculation using density functional theory (DFT) is costly: about 60% of Materials Project entries lack eDOS and over 80% lack phDOS. Existing crystal-to-spectrum models rely on pooled structural representations and pointwise regression, which blur spectral features and violate total-state conservation. Here we introduce DeepDOSReasoner, a physics-grounded framework that decodes spectra through atom-to-energy source attention and refines the normalized DOS through learned continuity-equation transport, enforcing nonnegativity and total-state conservation by construction. DeepDOSReasoner achieves the highest prediction accuracy across all evaluated metrics for both eDOS and phDOS, recovers element-resolved contributions without supervision, and most reliably identifies rare valence-band (VB) gaps. In a prospective screen of 8,106 uncharted materials validated by new DFT calculations, its predictions remain the most accurate among the methods evaluated, with 25 of its top 50 candidates confirmed as VB-gap materials. Three case studies spanning substituted compounds, photovoltaic chalcogenides, and a high-entropy alloy demonstrate transfer across materials classes. Ablations confirm that both stages are necessary, while the model retains comparable accuracy when trained on only 25% of the data. Predicting spectra in milliseconds enables exhaustive screening; we release predictions filling the missing eDOS for 91,405 and phDOS for 127,768 Materials Project entries. DeepDOSReasoner provides a blueprint for integrating machine learning with physical reasoning toward high-throughput spectral screening and autonomous materials discovery.

Lattice Thermal Conductivity of MgO and the MgSiO$_3$ Polymorphs Bridgmanite and Post-Perovskite at Lower-Mantle Conditions Using Fine-Tuned MACE Potentials

Amrendra Kumar Tiwari, Sandro Jahn

Heat flow across the core-mantle boundary (CMB) depends on the lattice thermal conductivity ($κ_{\mathrm{latt}}$) of lowermost-mantle minerals, yet calculated CMB values for MgO differ severalfold, and high-temperature estimates of the conductivity increase across the bridgmanite-post-perovskite transition range from about 20 to 50%. Direct solutions of the linearized phonon Boltzmann transport equation (LBTE) require thousands of density functional theory (DFT) force evaluations per thermodynamic state. Here we replace these evaluations with phase-specific, fine-tuned MACE potentials for MgO, MgSiO3 bridgmanite (Pv), and MgSiO3 post-perovskite (PPv). The potentials reproduce DFT energies, forces, stresses, pressure-volume relations, and compressed-state phonons for all three phases. For MgO at 0 GPa, replacing MACE forces with DFT forces in otherwise identical transport calculations changes $κ_{\mathrm{latt}}$ by 0.2% at 300 K and 1.9% at 2000 K. PPv is 44-55% more conductive than Pv at the same pressure and temperature and strongly anisotropic, with $κ_{[100]}/κ_{[010]}$ ranging from 1.7 to 2.1. The quasi-harmonic phase boundary has a Clapeyron slope of $(7.4 \pm 0.1)$ MPa/K and intersects a model geotherm at 116.2 GPa and 2660 K, where the transition raises the silicate conductivity by 47%, to 8.6 W/(m K). Heating in the basal thermal boundary layer lowers the MgO and PPv conductivities to 37.3 and 6.7 W/(m K) at the CMB, where Hashin-Shtrikman bounds of 9.5-11.4 W/(m K) for an Fe-free aggregate of 80 vol% silicate and 20 vol% MgO encompass the 10 W/(m K) used in earlier heat-flow estimates. The density-temperature parameterizations provide an intrinsic end-member reference for lower-mantle thermal models.

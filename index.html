Evidence-grounded large language models guide perovskite solar cell design and optimization

YanKai Chen, Zhi Wan, ZiHao Li, Tao Jing

Using large language models to interpret experimental evidence, design discriminating experiments and formulate well-supported mechanistic explanations remains a central challenge in materials research. Here we establish PSM-Bench, an 800-question benchmark developed by perovskite researchers to evaluate scientific reasoning in perovskite photovoltaics. We also introduce OpenPerov, an expert-informed language-model framework that learns from expert-calibrated evidence records reconstructed from 4,035 training articles; these records link experimental strategies, controls and observations to mechanistic interpretations. Structured-evidence adaptation outperformed conventional question-answer training, and OpenPerov Flash ranked first among 13 closed-book systems. OpenPerov Pro raised Scientific Mastery further, from 87.6 to 89.1, by revising selected passages with retrieved evidence. On questions derived from subsequent studies, both models also outscored expert-authored answers. In an experimental test of self-assembled monolayer design, OpenPerov selected a K analogue that outperformed the Na analogue preferred by experts. Devices incorporating the selected contact reached a power conversion efficiency of 27.05% and retained 96.74% of their initial efficiency after 1,000 h of dark thermal ageing at 85 $^\circ$C. These results show that reconstructed scientific reasoning provides an effective basis for building domain models that interpret experiments and inform materials design.

Synergistic Graphene and PEDOT:PSS Layers on Silicon for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability in Schottky Junction Solar Cells

Kentaro Nakajo, Hojun Im, Keiichiro Nishimura, Masahiro Teraoka, Masaya Kikuchi

While graphene/n-type silicon (Gr/n-Si) and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si solar cells offer a cost-effective alternative to conventional photovoltaics, achieving simultaneous high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability remains a formidable challenge. Here, we report the synergistic enhancement of both performance and stability in a planar trilayer PEDOT:PSS/Gr/n-Si Schottky junction architecture. By systematically benchmarking this hybrid device against pristine Gr/n-Si and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si reference cells fabricated on untextured substrates, we isolate the fundamental optoelectronic and barrier functions of the carbon lattice. Comprehensive analyses reveal that the PEDOT:PSS layer simultaneously functions as an anti-reflection coating, a moderate p-type dopant for the underlying graphene, and a conductivity enhancer, collectively optimizing interfacial charge transport. Consequently, the trilayer device achieves a PCE of 8.52%, markedly outperforming the pristine Gr/n-Si reference cell (4.56%). Crucially, the atomic impermeability of the graphene sheet serves as a robust environmental barrier. While bare PEDOT:PSS/n-Si junctions retained only ~29% of their initial efficiency after 75 days of ambient exposure, graphene shields the active junction from moisture-induced degradation of the hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS overlayer. This enables the trilayer device to retain approximately 90% of its initial efficiency over the same 75-day period. The complementary roles of graphene as both a highly crystalline charge transport interface and an impermeable protective barrier establish a highly effective strategy for engineering high-efficiency, environmentally stable hybrid photovoltaics.

Multiwavelength Photoluminescence as a Non-destructive Probe of Depth-Dependent UV Damage in Perovskite Semiconductors

Zixu Huang, Julisa Juarez, Akash Dasgupta, David S. Ginger

Buried interfaces play a critical role in the operational stability of perovskite optoelectronic devices, yet remain challenging to probe non-destructively. Here, we demonstrate a multiwavelength photoluminescence-based approach exploiting the wavelength-dependent absorption of perovskites, enabling fast, and non-destructive probing of depth-sensitive degradation behavior of perovskite devices under operational conditions. Using ultraviolet (UV) light-soaking with different indium tin oxide (ITO) modifiers as a test case, we employ wide-field photoluminescence (PL) imaging, time-resolved photoluminescence (trPL) and ideality factor measurements with different wavelength excitation to characterize degradation behavior of both perovskite thin films and photovoltaic devices under UV soaking. Using only nondestructive characterization, we show that UV-induced degradation in p-i-n perovskite solar cells occurs preferentially at the HTL interface, adversely affecting device stability, consistent with ex-situ destructive tests. Our results also show that modifying the buried interface with 3-mercaptopropyltrimethoxysilane reduces UV-induced damage at the buried interface, thus improving the UV stability of both perovskite films and devices. This work establishes a fast, non-destructive strategy to screen depth-dependent degradation in perovskite devices, offering insights for interface design and stability optimization.

Provenance-grouped protocol for evaluating machine learning in perovskite photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells are typically evaluated using random data splits, which we show systematically leak information through massive internal duplication. Because nearly two-thirds of curated devices share their exact recipe with another, random splits primarily reward the mere recall of historical data rather than testing true physical generalisation. To solve this, we mapped the institutional provenance of the Perovskite Database and established a rigorous hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications, and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models establishes new state-of-the-art performance across all splits and reveals a critical regime shift: while tree ensembles dominate random splits by recognising familiar recipes, their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures excel. Furthermore, feature importance demonstrates that the device architecture and fabrication methodology dictate final efficiency just as strongly as the absorber chemistry. This standardised protocol empowers the community to separate memorisation from true physical discovery.

Stable AgBiS2 near-infrared absorbers for thin film tandem photovoltaics

Shiling Dong, Yongjie Wang, Hae-Jun Seok, Mengyuan Huang, Edward Newton, Der Ying Chen

AgBiS2 has rapidly risen in efficiency as single-junction photovoltaics (PVs), but its potential for tandem PVs is unexplored. Herein, we perform optical modeling based on the experimental absorption spectrum of AgBiS2, revealing that, as the NIR sub-cell, efficiencies exceeding 47.9% are achievable in triple-junction tandems, superseding tandems using Pb/Sn perovskite NIR cells (35.9%). However, fulfilling this potential requires overcoming the limited transport properties of AgBiS2 nanocrystal films, which constrains film thicknesses to only 30-50 nm, severely restricting NIR response. A solution-phase ligand exchange process is herein developed, allowing one-step deposition of passivated AgBiS2 nanocrystals that maintain efficient charge-carrier extraction despite an order of magnitude increase in film thickness. These films enable 24.0%-efficient four-terminal tandems with visible-light-absorbing lead-halide perovskite sub-cells, and monolithic tandems successfully demonstrating voltage addition. AgBiS2 PVs have high stability, retaining >90% of the original efficiency after 3 months of storage in dry air without encapsulation. This work establishes the significant potential of AgBiS2 as a stable and scalable NIR absorber for tandem PVs.

High-Entropy A-Site Disorder Enhances Oxygen Evolution Reaction Activity and Stability in Nickelate Thin Films

Min-Ju Choi, Le Wang, Krishna Prasad Koirala, Hyoju Park, Mark E. Bowden, Elizabeth Helen Krenkel

Rare-earth nickelates are among the most active oxide electrocatalysts for the oxygen evolution reaction (OER), owing to their tunable Ni-O covalency, near-unity eg occupancy, and sensitivity to A-site chemistry, oxygen stoichiometry, and epitaxial strain. Here, we use epitaxial A-site high-entropy (La0.2Pr0.2Nd0.2Sm0.2Eu0.2)NiO3 thin films to reveal how epitaxial strain influences defect formation, Ni valence, electronic structure, and OER activity in a chemically disordered rare-earth (RE) sublattice. Tensile strain promotes Ruddlesden-Popper fault formation, interrupts the corner-sharing NiO6 network, and lowers the average Ni oxidation state. Although the occupied density of states near the Fermi level remains largely unchanged, O K-edge X-ray absorption spectroscopy reveals increased low-energy unoccupied states under tensile strain. First-principles calculations suggest that tensile strain shifts RE 4f-derived states toward the Fermi level, enhancing RE 4f-O 2p-Ni 3d hybridization. These coupled structural and electronic changes lead to earlier OER onset, stronger pre-catalytic Ni redox features, and higher activity than compressively strained films and representative single-A-site nickelates. The high-entropy nickelate also shows enhanced resistance to OER-induced structural degradation compared with LaNiO3. These results suggest that A-site configurational disorder and epitaxial strain can jointly tune defect formation and rare-earth-derived electronic states, providing a route for improving perovskite oxide electrocatalysts.

Controlling nitrogen incorporation in (Eu,Ba,Ca)TiO$_3$-derived perovskite-type oxynitrides through Fermi Level Engineering

Margarida Barroso, Marie Neumann, Rachele Sciotto, Shama Perween, Magdalena O. Cichocka, Jefferson Bettini

Defect states directly influence not only functional properties, but also oxynitride formation during ammonolysis. Along with structural stability and charge balance, these defects can cause non-uniform nitrogen incorporation. To address this, the recently proposed Fermi Level Engineering concept provides a powerful alternative of rationalising oxynitride synthesis. By using the electron chemical potential, i.e., the Fermi level, as a unique descriptor, this approach can predict, understand, and manipulate defect formation enthalpies and charge transition levels, subsequently allowing to incorporate defect-property correlations to the concept. This work combines theoretical modelling and experimental characterisation of barium titanium oxynitrides to explain an intrinsic, self-limiting nitridation process. Progressive nitrogen incorporation causes a Fermi level downward shift, leaving further nitrogen substitution on oxygen sites energetically unfavourable, up to 0.03 N per unit formula. We demonstrate that A-site cation substitution in Eu$_{1-x}$Ba$_x$TiO$_3$- and Eu$_{1-x}$Ca$_x$TiO$_3$-derived oxynitrides can be applied to control compensation of the additional negative charge from N$^{3-}$ and keep the Fermi level high. The Eu$^{2+/3+}$ redox couple enables substitution of oxygen by nitrogen through pinning of the Fermi level at the europium charge transition level, at $\sim$ 2.9 eV above the VBM. This allows for higher nitrogen uptake of up to 0.65 per formula unit (taken from hot gas extraction) through a more controlled process. Further incorporation is then limited by an intra-Ti(O,N)$_6$ octahedra distortion due to internal bond-angle variance. These findings demonstrate that Fermi Level Engineering provides a pathway to predict and overcome intrinsic synthetic and thermodynamic limitations in oxynitride synthesis.

Controlling Surface Chemistry in Tin-Lead Perovskites for High-Efficiency Solar Cells

Lijun Chen, Sitong Zhao, Gaukhar Nigmetova, David Garcia Romero, Matteo Pitaro, Sofia Lerda

Interfacial nonradiative recombination limits tin-lead perovskite photovoltaics. Here we show that neutral ethylenediamine chelates undercoordinated metals at tin-rich surfaces, weakens metal-iodide bonds, and removes excess tin, reconstructing the surface towards a stoichiometric tin-to-lead ratio. In contrast, protonated ethylenediammonium iodide initially passivates undercoordinated metal sites before undergoing defect-assisted deprotonation. Atomistic simulations reveal that subsurface iodide vacancies promote iodide migration and lower the energetic cost of deprotonation. Ethylenediamine-induced reconstruction produces stronger n-type surface character, facilitates electron extraction into C60, and suppresses interfacial nonradiative recombination, enabling methylammonium-free solar cells with a power conversion efficiency of 25.86%. Together, these distinct surface reaction pathways and the defect-assisted evolution of the passivating species underscore the importance of understanding perovskite surface chemistry for the rational design of molecular passivation strategies.

Amorphous selenium-alloyed TeOx thin-film solar cells

Zhaoheng Ling, Linqu Luo, Chrysa Aivalioti, Temur Maksudov, Mengxi Zhang, Theodoulos Kalogerakis

Tellurium dioxide (TeO$_2$) is a wide-bandgap oxide with attractive processing versatility and emerging p-type semiconducting behaviour, making it a promising material system for optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications. However, TeO$_2$-derived thin films prepared by scalable manufacturing processes, such as vacuum sublimation, can contain residual metallic Te$^0$ species that introduce trap states, limit hole transport, and promote carrier recombination. Here, we demonstrate a selenium alloying strategy to control the defect chemistry of vacuum-sublimed TeO$_x$ films. TeO$_2$:Se powder mixtures (90:10~wt\%) were thermally evaporated and subsequently annealed in air at 225~$^\circ$C for up to 9~h, yielding amorphous TeO$_x$:Se films with an optical bandgap of 0.98~eV under optimised conditions. Structural, chemical, optical, and electrical analyses show that prolonged annealing promotes Se redistribution, suppresses residual Te$^0$ to approximately 0.4\%, reduces the film's surface roughness, and shifts the valence-band edge. To evaluate the potential of TeO$_x$:Se for photovoltaic applications, we fabricated thin-film solar cells with an ITO/ZnO/TeO$_x$:Se/MoO$_x$/Ag architecture. Devices based on an 80~nm TeO$_x$:Se active layer annealed for 9~h exhibit optimal performance, with broadband photocurrent generation from 300 to 1000~nm, yielding an open-circuit voltage of 0.50~V, a short-circuit current density of 8.50~mA~cm$^{-2}$, a fill factor of 0.44, and a maximum power conversion efficiency of approximately 1.87\%. These results establish selenium-alloyed TeO$_x$ as a viable oxide-derived absorber for thin-film photovoltaics and highlight chemical-state control as an effective route for improving photovoltaic performance.

High-fidelity training-free photovoltaic module mapping from multi-view UAV imagery

WenHao Duana, Qi Chena, Penglei Zhang, Ruizhe Deng, Zhiling Guo, Haoran Zhang

High-fidelity photovoltaic (PV) module mapping requires complete instance recovery and accurate delineation. Vision foundation models offer zero-shot segmentation, but converting incomplete and inconsistent predictions across views into a complete, geometrically accurate map of individual modules remains challenging. We propose the Multi-View PV Module Mapper (MV-PVMapper), a training-free framework coupling vision foundation models with unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) photogrammetry. First, Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM 3) generates candidates from original images using text prompts. Their contours are then transferred into object space through per-module slope correction to limit distortion from surface-model noise, and verified against representative module dimensions to guide cross-view instance selection. Finally, accepted instances return to all covering images as box prompts for SAM~3, supplementing text prompts with spatial cues for iterative segmentation, verification and fusion. On five-direction surveys of three operational rooftop PV plants at approximately 1 cm nadir ground sampling distance, two feedback iterations recover 7,710 of 7,711 reference modules with no false positives at an intersection-over-union (IoU) matching threshold of 0.5. Macro-averaged area precision and recall reach 99.00 % and 98.52 %, respectively. Averaged across sites, the aggregated Jaccard index reaches 97.55 %, compared with 60.73 % and 41.24 % for orthomosaic segmentation and multi-view pixel-voting baselines, respectively; average precision at the stricter IoU threshold of 0.95 reaches 95.67 %. Ablation studies show that module-size constraints are critical for instance separation, while slope correction and feedback to all covering views improve boundary accuracy. These results are achieved without task-specific image annotation or segmentation-model weight updates.

Diffusion-Based Generative Screening of ABX$_3$ Perovskite Compositions for Bandgap-Targeted Materials Discovery

Sunraj Patel, Krishanu Biswas

Crystal-generation models can explore compositions and atomic arrangements beyond a fixed set of crystal prototypes, but their usefulness for a specific materials family depends on whether the generated structures retain the defining topology and whether their predicted properties remain reliable across the sampled chemistry. Here, a pretrained diffusion model is used to generate candidate ABX$_3$ perovskites and to examine their structural, chemical and electronic-property distributions. A connectivity-based filter distinguishes three-dimensional corner-sharing perovskites from structures that share the same nominal ABX$_3$ stoichiometry. The accepted set contains substantial B-site multiplicity, including ordered double-perovskite configurations that are inaccessible to the two-prototype substitution reference used here. The generated set is enriched in chloride-, bromide- and iodide-containing compositions relative to the Materials Project, while all generated B-site elements are already represented in that database. On the downstream MP-trained ALIGNN scale, the predicted bandgap distribution is shifted towards 1.4 eV, but the target-window fraction (8.2\%) is close to the MP reference fraction (7.3\%); the separate MatterGen and ALIGNN label sources prevent this comparison from establishing target-condition enrichment. An ALIGNN model performs well on a formula-grouped random test split but does not outperform a constant baseline on a deliberately severe B-site-disjoint test. First-principles calculations on six generated structures show that relaxation can alter structural classification and that predicted and calculated gaps can differ appreciably. These results identify both the chemical-space coverage offered by crystal diffusion and the structural and predictive checks needed before its outputs can be treated as materials candidates.

AutoEnergyDesign: An LLM-Driven Agent for Automatic Energy System Design Optimization

Amedeo Buonanno, Salvatore Fabozzi, Maria Valenti

Designing building energy systems is a high-dimensional, mixed-integer search problem: when each design is evaluated by a year-long simulation, black-box optimizers typically need hundreds of evaluations. This paper presents AutoEnergyDesign, a framework in which a Large Language Model (LLM) agent reasons over previous simulation results, proposes configuration changes and learns in context from each outcome, while a git-based mechanism automatically reverts unsuccessful changes. The agent couples a reasoning model with an explicit memory organisation (procedural, semantic, episodic and reflective), a constrained action interface and an environment-grounded keep/revert signal. The simulator covers eight technologies (photovoltaics, micro-wind, combined heat and power, solar thermal, gas boilers, batteries, heat pumps and inverters) and computes the 25-year Net Present Cost (NPC), including capital and operating costs and salvage value. On a case study with measured building load profiles, the agent, run with Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 (ten runs of 50 simulations each), is compared with random search, Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) Bayesian optimization and a $(1+1)$ evolution strategy sharing the agent's acceptance rule (300 evaluations, ten seeds). With all three models the agent reaches an NPC of about 3.53 MEUR, 1.20% below the best TPE result and 0.23% below the best evolution-strategy result. It comes within 1% of this design after a median of 18 (Opus) to 27.5 (Haiku) simulations, whereas among the baselines only the evolution strategy does so, in half of its seeds and after a median of 192 evaluations. Opus reaches the best design in all ten runs, Sonnet in six (two runs never come within 1%), and Haiku comes within 1% in every run but reaches it exactly only once. Every decision is recorded in an auditable trace.

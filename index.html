Synergistic Graphene and PEDOT:PSS Layers on Silicon for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability in Schottky Junction Solar Cells

Kentaro Nakajo, Hojun Im, Keiichiro Nishimura, Masahiro Teraoka, Masaya Kikuchi

While graphene/n-type silicon (Gr/n-Si) and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si solar cells offer a cost-effective alternative to conventional photovoltaics, achieving simultaneous high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability remains a formidable challenge. Here, we report the synergistic enhancement of both performance and stability in a planar trilayer PEDOT:PSS/Gr/n-Si Schottky junction architecture. By systematically benchmarking this hybrid device against pristine Gr/n-Si and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si reference cells fabricated on untextured substrates, we isolate the fundamental optoelectronic and barrier functions of the carbon lattice. Comprehensive analyses reveal that the PEDOT:PSS layer simultaneously functions as an anti-reflection coating, a moderate p-type dopant for the underlying graphene, and a conductivity enhancer, collectively optimizing interfacial charge transport. Consequently, the trilayer device achieves a PCE of 8.52%, markedly outperforming the pristine Gr/n-Si reference cell (4.56%). Crucially, the atomic impermeability of the graphene sheet serves as a robust environmental barrier. While bare PEDOT:PSS/n-Si junctions retained only ~29% of their initial efficiency after 75 days of ambient exposure, graphene shields the active junction from moisture-induced degradation of the hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS overlayer. This enables the trilayer device to retain approximately 90% of its initial efficiency over the same 75-day period. The complementary roles of graphene as both a highly crystalline charge transport interface and an impermeable protective barrier establish a highly effective strategy for engineering high-efficiency, environmentally stable hybrid photovoltaics.

A Hybrid TFT-NBEATS Framework for Robust Multi-Horizon Photovoltaic Power Forecasting

Shafi Bin Sultan, Sabik Bin Sultan, Mohammad Tajrian Arafat Alvi, Rayhanul Islam Sony, Abdulah Al Numan

Accurate multi-horizon photovoltaic (PV) power forecasting is essential for grid stability, energy scheduling, and the efficient operation of PVbatterygrid systems. However, existing models often fail to jointly capture short-term variability and long-term temporal patterns, while lacking unified multi-horizon capability and interpretability. To address these limitations, this study proposes a novel hybrid deep learning framework that integrates a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) and N-BEATS within an attention-based fusion architecture. The proposed model leverages TFT to learn short-term temporal dependencies and dynamic feature importance, while N-BEATS captures long-term trend and seasonal components through interpretable basis decomposition. An adaptive attention mechanism dynamically fuses both branches, enabling balanced multi-scale learning. The framework simultaneously generates forecasts for 30-minute, 2-hour, 6-hour, 12-hour, and 24-hour horizons within a single unified pipeline. The model is evaluated using multi-year real-world datasets from the Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre (DKASC). Experimental results demonstrate superior performance over statistical, machine learning, and deep learning baselines. The proposed model achieves RMSE values of 42.35, 46.27, 52.29, and 48.11 across the 20162017, 2023, 2024, and 20252026 datasets, respectively, corresponding to a 3540% reduction compared to the best baseline (TFT). Additionally, MAPE remains below 5% across all datasets, indicating high predictive accuracy and stability. The model also provides interpretability through attention visualization and trendseasonality decomposition, and is deployed on a cloud-based platform for real-time forecasting. These results demonstrate that the proposed framework is robust, interpretable, and suitable for practical renewable energy applications.

Benchmark for Structured Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Perovskite Photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou, Gaoxiang Yuan

While retrieval-augmented language models increasingly serve as literature assistants in the physical sciences, existing benchmarks evaluate them primarily on qualitative text overlap or through model-based scoring rather than against verified experimental ground truth. In experimental materials disciplines such as perovskite photovoltaics, however, the most critical inquiries are inherently quantitative and aggregate measurements across dozens or hundreds of papers. Here, we present a quantitative question-answering benchmark where all 755 ground-truth answers are computed directly from 45,441 measured device records (40,889 human-curated records through 2021 and 4,552 records extracted from 2022-2026 literature). Benchmarking six retrieval architectures over 158,274 passages from 25,662 papers using an open 14B model on a single 24 GB GPU, standard passage retrieval tops out at 16-23% accuracy (compared to 1.6-6.5% zero-shot). In contrast, equipping the language model with read-only SQL execution over the structured device table elevates accuracy to 51.5% on historical and 50.8% on prospective literature -- a 33.01.8 percentage-point advantage over hybrid text retrieval. Aggregate queries spanning extrema, distributions, and filtered record counts prove virtually unsolvable via text retrieval alone, demonstrating that systematic table extraction is an operational prerequisite for literature-scale quantitative inquiry.

High-Throughput Imaging of Degradation-Inducing Microscopic Impurities in Perovskite Solar Cells

Sofiia Kosar, Anil R. Pininti, Vladyslav Hnapovskyi, José P. Jurado, Subhashri Mannar, Lorenzo Mardegan

The scalable fabrication of high-quality, large-area perovskite thin films is hindered by microscopic inhomogeneities, particularly residual compositional impurities formed during processing. Identifying these impurities, understanding their impact on device operation, and enabling their rapid detection are essential for upscaling perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, nano-Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy combined with high-resolution optical and scanning probe techniques was used to identify detrimental impurities in wide-bandgap perovskite films relevant to tandem solar cells. Photo-stress experiments revealed that degradation of the perovskite layer initiates at impurity-perovskite interfaces, demonstrating that impurities act as failure nucleation sites. Leveraging these insights, a rapid, non-invasive, and high-throughput framework based on high-resolution reflected light microscopy and machine learning-supported image analysis was developed, enabling detection and quantification of harmful impurities in as-prepared films within seconds. Films with higher impurity density show accelerated early degradation, establishing this parameter as an early-warning metric for stability screening. Extending this framework to degradation tracking further reveals coupled photo- and thermo-chemical contributions to impurity-mediated instability. Overall, this work establishes a practical chemically-validated diagnostic imaging framework for rapid pre-screening of perovskite films to enable stable and scalable PSCs.

Provenance-grouped protocol for evaluating machine learning in perovskite photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells are typically evaluated using random data splits, which we show systematically leak information through massive internal duplication. Because nearly two-thirds of curated devices share their exact recipe with another, random splits primarily reward the mere recall of historical data rather than testing true physical generalisation. To solve this, we mapped the institutional provenance of the Perovskite Database and established a rigorous hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications, and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models establishes new state-of-the-art performance across all splits and reveals a critical regime shift: while tree ensembles dominate random splits by recognising familiar recipes, their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures excel. Furthermore, feature importance demonstrates that the device architecture and fabrication methodology dictate final efficiency just as strongly as the absorber chemistry. This standardised protocol empowers the community to separate memorisation from true physical discovery.

Foundation-Style Pretraining for Scalable and Transferable Distributed PV Forecasting

Dayin Chen, Xiaodan Shi, Xin Xu, Zhongyue Su, Shibo Zhu, Ruizhe Deng

The rapid growth of distributed photovoltaic (PV) generation is increasing the demand for accurate forecasting across large numbers of geographically dispersed stations. Existing approaches are commonly developed for individual sites or specific forecasting horizons, requiring repeated training and maintenance as new stations are integrated and limiting direct reuse at previously unseen sites. To address this scalability gap, this paper presents a domain-specific foundation-style pretraining framework that learns a shared forecasting model from heterogeneous multi-site and multi-horizon PV data. NWP-assisted forecasting is formulated as a sequence-completion problem that unifies historical PV power, meteorological observations, future numerical weather prediction (NWP) forecasts, and time encodings. Three pretraining objectives are designed and systematically evaluated, including forecast-aligned reconstruction, generic reconstruction, and mixed reconstruction. The mixed objective combines the operational structure of PV forecasting with random sequence reconstruction and provides the most balanced performance across forecasting accuracy, degraded NWP conditions, and downstream adaptation. Experiments on 479 distributed PV stations use 340 stations for model development and 139 spatially held-out stations for zero-shot evaluation. Across horizons from 6 hours to 7 days, the multi-site pretrained MR model outperforms representative station-specific and shared baselines, as well as generalist time-series models, including a recent large-scale pretrained foundation model. The pretrained model can also be directly deployed at new PV stations and subsequently adapted to region-specific operational objectives when local data become available, providing a scalable forecasting paradigm for large distributed PV fleets.

K. Segadlo, O. J. J. Ronsin, J. Harting

A limited understanding of how evaporation couples to crystallization pathways hampers control over the formation of solution-processed perovskite photoactive layers. Identifying these mechanisms-and learning how to select and tune them-is essential for achieving the desired crystal morphology and, ultimately, improving solar-cell performance and stability. To investigate crystallization pathways in evaporating thin films, we develop a simple and general zero-dimensional population-balance model that couples evaporation, chemical reactions, and crystallization. We use the model to explore a range of parameters for a solvent-solute system that follows a two-step crystallization pathway: solvent evaporation first induces formation of a crystalline precursor, which subsequently recrystallizes into the final phase. The model predicts the evolution of the film composition during drying and provides quantitative LaMer curves. Based on these results, we introduce a vocabulary of "supply limitations" and "rate limitations" that enables the interpretation of crystallization kinetics in terms of the governing process conditions.

Magnetoelectric Bulk Photovoltaic Effect in Centrosymmetric Solids

Longjun Xiang, Fuming Xu, Lei Zhang, Jian Wang

It is commonly believed that a bulk photovoltaic current requires broken spatial inversion symmetry. However, in this work, we demonstrate that dc photocurrents can arise in centrosymmetric solids from the bilinear coupling between the electric and magnetic components of light. This magnetoelectric bulk photovoltaic effect (MBPE) has a quantum-geometric origin in the dipole moments of the Zeeman quantum metric and Zeeman Berry curvature. We identify four distinct MBPE mechanisms, including time-reversal-even and time-reversal-odd injection-like and shift-like photocurrents driven by linearly or circularly polarized light. Our symmetry analysis shows that the MBPE is allowed in broad classes of two-dimensional centrosymmetric nonmagnetic and magnetic materials under normally incident light. As representative examples, we predict sizable MBPEs in centrosymmetric monolayer Pt$_2$HgSe$_3$ and altermagnetic RuF$_4$, with current densities on the order of $1~\mathrm{mA/m}$. Notably, the injection-like photocurrent in RuF$_4$ reverses sign upon switching either the light helicity or the Néel vector. Our results extend the bulk photovoltaic effect to centrosymmetric solids and open new opportunities for electrical readout of altermagnetic order.

How Memristors Work: A Step-by-Step Guide to the CALM Model

This tutorial shows how a single minimal model, the conductance-activated quasi-linear memristor (CALM) model, explains all these features with three ingredients: the two conductance states, the rectification function x_eq (u) and the characteristic time tau(u). The tutorial is a slide presentation that the reader can follow alone, with exercises designed to be solved with the help of an AI assistant, reference Python code, and this support text. The text can also be read on its own: Section 4 gives a compact account of the content of the slides, with the main equations and figures, and the solutions to the exercises are given at the end.

Collectively, these results further establish multiwavelength excitation PL as a platform for studying recombination losses during aging of perovskite films.

Multi-property Feynman-Kac Steering of Crystal Diffusion Models

Oded Mousai, Sapir Esther Yiflach, Barak Itzchak Davidovitch, Yuval Mualem, Sarit Kraus, Hannah-Noa Barad

Pretrained diffusion models provide powerful priors for generating new materials, including inorganic crystals, but generating useful materials requires satisfying specific chemical and physical properties, from mechanical stiffness to bandgaps. Existing approaches either retrain models for new target properties or steer only the atom coordinates and lattice during generation, leaving composition unchanged despite its fundamental role in determining physical properties. We present FK-ReMask, an inference-time, gradient-free method that steers both the continuous and discrete channels of a pretrained crystal diffusion model. During generation, FK-ReMask maintains multiple denoising trajectories and replicates promising candidates using Feynman-Kac particle selection. However, selection alone is insufficient: in the atom-type channel, committed elements cannot be revised, so cloning propagates existing compositions and limits the population's ability to explore new ones. We address this with an atom re-masking mechanism, which reopens committed sites during the generation process and allows alternative compositions to emerge. Across four electronic, photovoltaic, and mechanical objectives, our method improves the yield of SUN (stable, unique, novel) and property-on-target materials. We further demonstrate joint steering of three photovoltaic properties, showing that multiple physical objectives can be specified entirely at inference time without retraining.

Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System for extrapolative prediction of perovskite solar cells

Existing machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells struggle to predict devices made from new materials. Most treat device layers as arbitrary categorical labels and represent perovskite absorbers either through featureless composition vectors or through rigid crystal graphs that require a pre-solved unit cell. As a result, they cannot generalize to unrecorded alloys or unfamiliar cell architectures. We present the Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System (V-PaSS), an architecture built around the physical continuity of perovskite solid solutions. V-PaSS represents arbitrary mixed-cation, mixed-halide absorbers by interpolating relaxed crystal graphs of 23 pure $ABX_3$ endpoint compounds via Vegard's law with learned bandgap bowing. V-PaSS pulls furthest ahead where test devices differ most from training: reducing error by 15.7-19.0% on unseen absorber alloys, by 16.1% on mixed absorbers from new laboratories, and widening its lead over the baseline by 3.6$\times$ on devices with unobserved transport layers. Combined with tree ensembles, V-PaSS sets a new state of the art across all five benchmark splits.

This work proposes an exciton-kinetics coupled drift-diffusion (EKDD) model that bridges device-level charge transport with singlet and triplet excited-state kinetics. State populations and reversible photophysical transitions are solved self-consistently, enabling transient and steady-state device response calculations. We investigate how LE-CT, CT-CS, and LE-T energy offsets, together with triplet-transfer kinetics, govern charge generation, recombination, voltage loss, and extraction in organic photovoltaics. We identify CT-CS offset as a primary, largely overlooked factor controlling charge generation and voltage loss. A positive offset makes CT3-CS separation exothermic and improves generation, but lowers CS energy and increases voltage loss; a negative offset makes separation endothermic and highly temperature-sensitive. The model reproduces triplet-mediated recombination in PM6:Y6 and material-dependent triplet recycling in D18:NFA blends. We propose design rules for balanced generation and voltage loss via two charge-transfer channels: the LE-CT-CS pathway favors a moderate LE-CT offset of about 0.15-0.20 eV and a weakly exothermic CT-CS offset of about 0.05-0.075 eV; the T-CT3-CS pathway requires the same CT-CS alignment plus a small CT-T offset, no more than about 0.1 eV, corresponding to a recycling factor K greater than about 10^-2, to maintain fast T-to-CT3 recycling. CT-CS alignment is the main determinant of the charge-generation-voltage-loss trade-off, while triplet-recycling kinetics determines whether this balance survives at the device level. This work provides a unified theoretical description from exciton generation to free charge collection, offering a convenient tool for transient and steady-state analysis in organic photovoltaics.

The integration of two-dimensional Ti3C2Tx MXenes into Halide Perovskite (HaP) optoelectronics has shown immense potential for enhancing device performance, yet the fundamental chemical interactions within the precursor ink (a critical stage determining the final material state) remain largely unexplored. In stark contrast, methylammonium iodide (MAI) exhibits a stabilizing effect: it adsorbs onto the MXene surface via hydrogen bonding, effectively passivating the flakes and retarding degradation. In addition, our results reveal that the sequence of precursor addition governs the MXene surface state, requiring extra caution, but also enabling further tunability. These findings provide a critical view on how MXene integration with materials, in general, and HaP in particular, should be perceived, providing a fundamental for their rational engineering.

The extrinsic bulk photovoltaic effect from asymmetric electron-defect scattering can make an important contribution to the total photocurrent and is highly diagnostic of defect type. We present an ab initio Boltzmann framework combining optical excitation with electron-phonon and symmetric and skew electron-defect scattering, and we apply it to monolayer GeS. Because the defects that generate the current also relax the anisotropy that sustains it, the response peaks at a finite defect fraction. Within the scattering-limited model, cooling to $0$~K shifts the peak position toward lower defect fractions and yields a maximum enhancement of more than six orders of magnitude at fixed defect fraction. At selected low-temperature defect fractions, the total extrinsic current can exceed the intrinsic shift current by more than an order of magnitude, so that the defect fraction itself becomes a tunable parameter.

Combined photoluminescence and electrical characterization of valley photovoltaic devices explained with an equivalent circuit

Daixi Xia, Abhinav S. Sharma, Andreas Pusch, Murad J. Y. Tayebjee, Michael P. Nielsen, Fiacre E. Rougieux

We present an equivalent-circuit analysis that simultaneously considers the photoluminescence and electrical responses of valley photovoltaic devices. Valley photovoltaics (VPV) are a novel concept for hot-carrier solar cells, where intervalley scattering of electrons in the absorbing material helps maintain the conduction-band electron populations at metastable satellite valleys, which are at energies higher than the conduction-band minimum. We measure intensity- and voltage-dependent photoluminescence and electrical signals of a VPV device, which has previously shown S-shaped current-voltage characteristics and thus low efficiency. We present an equivalent circuit that captures the S-shaped current-voltage curves and simultaneously describes our photoluminescence and electric measurements. The equivalent-circuit model indicates that a reverse diode causes inefficient extraction of electrons and thus the S shape. We use a Poisson/drift-diffusion model to demonstrate that the reverse diode can be either explained by the valley-scattering process itself or a heterojunction barrier. The reverse diode must be eliminated before VPV devices can achieve high efficiency.

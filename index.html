Synergistic Graphene and PEDOT:PSS Layers on Silicon for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability in Schottky Junction Solar Cells

Kentaro Nakajo, Hojun Im, Keiichiro Nishimura, Masahiro Teraoka, Masaya Kikuchi

While graphene/n-type silicon (Gr/n-Si) and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si solar cells offer a cost-effective alternative to conventional photovoltaics, achieving simultaneous high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability remains a formidable challenge. Here, we report the synergistic enhancement of both performance and stability in a planar trilayer PEDOT:PSS/Gr/n-Si Schottky junction architecture. By systematically benchmarking this hybrid device against pristine Gr/n-Si and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si reference cells fabricated on untextured substrates, we isolate the fundamental optoelectronic and barrier functions of the carbon lattice. Comprehensive analyses reveal that the PEDOT:PSS layer simultaneously functions as an anti-reflection coating, a moderate p-type dopant for the underlying graphene, and a conductivity enhancer, collectively optimizing interfacial charge transport. Consequently, the trilayer device achieves a PCE of 8.52%, markedly outperforming the pristine Gr/n-Si reference cell (4.56%). Crucially, the atomic impermeability of the graphene sheet serves as a robust environmental barrier. While bare PEDOT:PSS/n-Si junctions retained only ~29% of their initial efficiency after 75 days of ambient exposure, graphene shields the active junction from moisture-induced degradation of the hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS overlayer. This enables the trilayer device to retain approximately 90% of its initial efficiency over the same 75-day period. The complementary roles of graphene as both a highly crystalline charge transport interface and an impermeable protective barrier establish a highly effective strategy for engineering high-efficiency, environmentally stable hybrid photovoltaics.

High-Throughput Imaging of Degradation-Inducing Microscopic Impurities in Perovskite Solar Cells

Sofiia Kosar, Anil R. Pininti, Vladyslav Hnapovskyi, José P. Jurado, Subhashri Mannar, Lorenzo Mardegan

The scalable fabrication of high-quality, large-area perovskite thin films is hindered by microscopic inhomogeneities, particularly residual compositional impurities formed during processing. Identifying these impurities, understanding their impact on device operation, and enabling their rapid detection are essential for upscaling perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, nano-Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy combined with high-resolution optical and scanning probe techniques was used to identify detrimental impurities in wide-bandgap perovskite films relevant to tandem solar cells. Photo-stress experiments revealed that degradation of the perovskite layer initiates at impurity-perovskite interfaces, demonstrating that impurities act as failure nucleation sites. Leveraging these insights, a rapid, non-invasive, and high-throughput framework based on high-resolution reflected light microscopy and machine learning-supported image analysis was developed, enabling detection and quantification of harmful impurities in as-prepared films within seconds. Films with higher impurity density show accelerated early degradation, establishing this parameter as an early-warning metric for stability screening. Extending this framework to degradation tracking further reveals coupled photo- and thermo-chemical contributions to impurity-mediated instability. Overall, this work establishes a practical chemically-validated diagnostic imaging framework for rapid pre-screening of perovskite films to enable stable and scalable PSCs.

Unifying Low-Frequency Raman and Infrared Signatures across Perovskites

Ethan Berger, Erik Fransson, Apinya Ngoipala, Lucas Svensson, Priyanka Deswal, Mehmet Baskurt

Perovskites have been studied for a wide range of applications, from oxide-based ferroelectrics to halide-perovskite photovoltaics. Despite their diverse functionalities, these materials share strong lattice anharmonicity arising from structural instabilities such as polar off-centering and octahedral tilting. These anharmonic distortions strongly influence the low-frequency vibrational response and can be observed in infrared (IR) and Raman spectra. However, low-frequency IR and Raman signatures are typically investigated separately, hindering the development of a unified understanding of vibrational spectroscopy across perovskite classes. In this work, we use machine-learning-based molecular dynamics to simulate the IR and Raman spectra of oxide perovskites (SrTiO$_3$ and BaTiO$_3$), halide perovskites (CsPbBr$_3$, CsSnBr$_3$, and CsGeBr$_3$) and double halide perovskites (Cs$_2$AgAlBr$_6$ and Cs$_2$AgBiBr$_6$). By comparing materials exhibiting polar, tilt-driven and mixed instabilities, we find that polar off-centering leads to enhanced low-frequency IR and dielectric responses, whereas nonpolar modes, particularly octahedral tilting, are associated with Raman central peaks. The strength of the low-frequency features also depends on how strongly the underlying modes couple to polarization or dielectric susceptibility. Finally, we show that the dynamics of these instabilities play an important role, with slow polar reorientations shifting the dielectric response to very low frequencies and overdamped tilting modes producing persistent Raman central peaks. These results establish a unified framework connecting low-frequency IR and Raman spectra to the symmetry, coupling and dynamics of their underlying anharmonic lattice instabilities.

Provenance-grouped protocol for evaluating machine learning in perovskite photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells are typically evaluated using random data splits, which we show systematically leak information through massive internal duplication. Because nearly two-thirds of curated devices share their exact recipe with another, random splits primarily reward the mere recall of historical data rather than testing true physical generalisation. To solve this, we mapped the institutional provenance of the Perovskite Database and established a rigorous hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications, and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models establishes new state-of-the-art performance across all splits and reveals a critical regime shift: while tree ensembles dominate random splits by recognising familiar recipes, their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures excel. Furthermore, feature importance demonstrates that the device architecture and fabrication methodology dictate final efficiency just as strongly as the absorber chemistry. This standardised protocol empowers the community to separate memorisation from true physical discovery.

A Hybrid TFT-NBEATS Framework for Robust Multi-Horizon Photovoltaic Power Forecasting

Shafi Bin Sultan, Sabik Bin Sultan, Mohammad Tajrian Arafat Alvi, Rayhanul Islam Sony, Abdulah Al Numan

Accurate multi-horizon photovoltaic (PV) power forecasting is essential for grid stability, energy scheduling, and the efficient operation of PVbatterygrid systems. However, existing models often fail to jointly capture short-term variability and long-term temporal patterns, while lacking unified multi-horizon capability and interpretability. To address these limitations, this study proposes a novel hybrid deep learning framework that integrates a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) and N-BEATS within an attention-based fusion architecture. The proposed model leverages TFT to learn short-term temporal dependencies and dynamic feature importance, while N-BEATS captures long-term trend and seasonal components through interpretable basis decomposition. An adaptive attention mechanism dynamically fuses both branches, enabling balanced multi-scale learning. The framework simultaneously generates forecasts for 30-minute, 2-hour, 6-hour, 12-hour, and 24-hour horizons within a single unified pipeline. The model is evaluated using multi-year real-world datasets from the Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre (DKASC). Experimental results demonstrate superior performance over statistical, machine learning, and deep learning baselines. The proposed model achieves RMSE values of 42.35, 46.27, 52.29, and 48.11 across the 20162017, 2023, 2024, and 20252026 datasets, respectively, corresponding to a 3540% reduction compared to the best baseline (TFT). Additionally, MAPE remains below 5% across all datasets, indicating high predictive accuracy and stability. The model also provides interpretability through attention visualization and trendseasonality decomposition, and is deployed on a cloud-based platform for real-time forecasting. These results demonstrate that the proposed framework is robust, interpretable, and suitable for practical renewable energy applications.

While retrieval-augmented language models increasingly serve as literature assistants in the physical sciences, existing benchmarks evaluate them primarily on qualitative text overlap or through model-based scoring rather than against verified experimental ground truth. In experimental materials disciplines such as perovskite photovoltaics, however, the most critical inquiries are inherently quantitative and aggregate measurements across dozens or hundreds of papers. Here, we present a quantitative question-answering benchmark where all 755 ground-truth answers are computed directly from 45,441 measured device records (40,889 human-curated records through 2021 and 4,552 records extracted from 2022-2026 literature). Benchmarking six retrieval architectures over 158,274 passages from 25,662 papers using an open 14B model on a single 24 GB GPU, standard passage retrieval tops out at 16-23% accuracy (compared to 1.6-6.5% zero-shot). Aggregate queries spanning extrema, distributions, and filtered record counts prove virtually unsolvable via text retrieval alone, demonstrating that systematic table extraction is an operational prerequisite for literature-scale quantitative inquiry.

Universal Synthesis of Large-Area Hybrid Perovskite Monolayers Enabling Lithography-Free, Low-Static-Power Optoelectronics

Shankeerthan Kalyanasundaram, Swarit Dwivedi, Sanje Mahasivam, Christopher R. McNeill, Sudha Mokkapati

Solution-phase synthesis of monolayer hybrid organic-inorganic perovskites is constrained by rapid out-of-plane crystal growth, uncontrolled nucleation, and strong interlayer aggregation, yielding flakes of only a few μm. We introduce a steric-engineering ternary-solvent growth strategy to synthesize monolayer and few-layer A2MX4 hybrid perovskites with exceptionally large surface areas. By combining alcohol cosolvents with an amine additive, out-of-plane stacking is suppressed. Using (PEA)2CuCl4 as a model system, monolayers with lateral areas exceeding 1000 μm2 are achieved, representing a massive dimensional increase over conventional methods. Molecular dynamics and quantum-chemical calculations show that longer-chain alcohols reorganize precursor solvation, while solvent volatility prolongs edge-mediated precursor transport during annealing. This strategy demonstrates universal applicability across diverse organic spacer cations (PEA+, F-PEA+, PentA+), metal centers (Cu2+, Mn2+, Pb2+), and halides (Cl-, Br-, I-). The large lateral dimensions enable direct, solvent-free electrical contact fabrication via physical shadow masking, bypassing chemical degradation from solvents used in conventional lithography. Planar devices fabricated from isolated ultrathin (PEA)2PbI4 channels exhibit ultra-low dark currents down to the 10^-15 - 10^-13 A range, enabling femtowatt-to-picowatt static power consumption, ~ 0.2 pW at 5 V, a 250-fold reduction relative to drop-cast films, while maintaining a high light-to-dark current ratio of 4.3 x 102. This work establishes precise steric and solvation control as a paradigm for scalable, compositionally diverse, low-static-power monolayer 2D perovskite optoelectronics.

Vision Transformers for Estimating Irradiance Using Data Scarce Sky Images

David Hamlyn, Sunny Chaudhary, Tasmiat Rahman

Accurate estimation of diffuse horizontal irradiance (DHI) is critical for optimising photovoltaic system performance and energy forecasting yet remains challenging in regions lacking comprehensive ground-based instrumentation. Recent advancements using Vision Transformers (ViTs) trained on extensive sky image datasets have shown promise in replacing costly irradiance measurement equipment, but the scarcity of long-term, high-quality sky imagery significantly restricts practical implementation. Addressing this critical gap, this study proposes a novel dual-framework approach designed for data-scarce scenarios. First, calculated atmospheric parameters, including extraterrestrial irradiance and cyclic time encodings, are integrated to represent sky conditions without utilising any instrumentation. Next, a sequential pipeline initially predicts synthetic global horizontal irradiance (GHI) and uses it as a feature, to refine DHI estimation. Finally, a dual-parallel architecture simultaneously processes raw and overlay-enhanced fisheye sky images. Overlays are generated through unsupervised, physics-informed cloud segmentation to highlight dynamic sky features. Empirical validation is performed using data from the Chilbolton Observatory, chosen for its temperate climate and frequent cloud variability. To simulate data-scarce conditions, models are trained on a single month (e.g., January) and evaluated across a temporally disjoint, full-year test set. Under this setup, the sequential and dual-parallel frameworks achieve RMSE values within 2-3 W/m^2 and 1-6 W/m^2, respectively, of a state-of-the-art ViT trained on the complete dataset. By combining physics-informed modelling with unsupervised segmentation, the proposed method provides a scalable and cost-effective solution for DHI estimation, advancing solar resource assessment in data-constrained environments.

Julisa Juarez, Akash Dasgupta, Alan Zhan, Lamtiur P. Samosir, David S. Ginger*

Studying real-time degradation in perovskite semiconductors remains an important challenge. Conventional methods often cannot separate interfacial and bulk processes or rely on destructive analysis. Here, we demonstrate multiwavelength excitation photoluminescence (PL) as a nondestructive approach to distinguish interface- and bulk-dominated recombination losses. Using phenethylammonium tetrafluoroborate (PEABF4) treatment as a model, we use PL to show that PEABF4 passivates the electron transport layer (ETL)/perovskite interface, reducing interfacial recombination losses from fullerene deposition. However, upon thermal aging, PEABF4-treated stacks degrade more rapidly, with recombination losses broadening from the ETL/perovskite interface into the absorber. Diffusion-recombination simulations support interpreting excitation-wavelength-dependent PL decay signatures consistent with changes in surface recombination velocity and bulk lifetime, providing a framework for depth-sensitive comparisons during aging. Collectively, these results further establish multiwavelength excitation PL as a platform for studying recombination losses during aging of perovskite films.

Optically bistable materials promise technological advances in optical information processing, memristive devices, and multiparametric sensing. Here, we report on Ho3+-activated nanocrystals that exhibit cavity-free intrinsic optical bistability (IOB) at ambient conditions. Non-thermal IOB stems from a cross-coupled photon avalanche (XPA) mechanism enabled by a low-phonon-energy double-perovskite host, which reduces nonradiative losses and facilitates excited-state population via XPA, even when the pump intensity is below threshold. IOB with high-contrast photoswitching (up to 46 dB) is observed at room temperature across multiple visible emission lines, enabling wavelength-division multiplexing and compatibility with common detectors. IOB is preserved across nano- to microscale crystals and different double-perovskite compositions, broadly enabling information photonics and advanced sensing.

Flexo-Photoresponses from First Principles

Zhichao Guo, Hua Wang, Haowei Xu

Many functionally important materials, such as silicon, possess high-symmetry crystal structures that forbid even-order nonlinear optical responses, thereby limiting their application in e.g., photonics and optoelectronics. Strain gradients can effectively break symmetries, activating otherwise forbidden optical responses. Although such flexo-photoresponses have attracted considerable interest recently, a comprehensive microscopic theoretical framework is still lacking. Here, we develop a first-principles approach that can systematically evaluate a wide range of flexo-photoresponses in realistic materials. By mapping strain gradients to long-wavelength phonons within the established theory of electron-phonon coupling, our approach captures the nonlocal quantum-mechanical effects of lattice deformations on nonlinear optical responses. Applying the framework to prototypical centrosymmetric materials, including Si, cubic $\rm SrTiO_3$, and monolayer black phosphorus, we demonstrate that a strain gradients of $10^5\sim10^6~\rm m^{-1}$ can induce flexo-second-harmonic generation and flexo-bulk photovoltaic effects comparable to the intrinsic responses of non-centrosymmetric crystals. Our work establishes a rigorous foundation for strain gradient engineering of light-matter interactions, opening new avenues for nanoscale optomechanics, optoelectronics, and beyond.

Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System for extrapolative prediction of perovskite solar cells

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Existing machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells struggle to predict devices made from new materials. Most treat device layers as arbitrary categorical labels and represent perovskite absorbers either through featureless composition vectors or through rigid crystal graphs that require a pre-solved unit cell. As a result, they cannot generalize to unrecorded alloys or unfamiliar cell architectures. We present the Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System (V-PaSS), an architecture built around the physical continuity of perovskite solid solutions. V-PaSS represents arbitrary mixed-cation, mixed-halide absorbers by interpolating relaxed crystal graphs of 23 pure $ABX_3$ endpoint compounds via Vegard's law with learned bandgap bowing. For the device stack, it couples layer sequence and cell polarity (n-i-p or p-i-n) with interfacial band offsets and encodes chemical descriptions with a scientific language model. Evaluated under an institution-grouped protocol where test devices come exclusively from unseen laboratories, an untuned default configuration of V-PaSS achieves the lowest error among fourteen tested models (2.861% MAE, a 5.2% relative reduction over a gradient-boosted baseline at 3.017%, and outperforming the best tuned benchmark at 2.912%). V-PaSS pulls furthest ahead where test devices differ most from training: reducing error by 15.7-19.0% on unseen absorber alloys, by 16.1% on mixed absorbers from new laboratories, and widening its lead over the baseline by 3.6$\times$ on devices with unobserved transport layers. Combined with tree ensembles, V-PaSS sets a new state of the art across all five benchmark splits.

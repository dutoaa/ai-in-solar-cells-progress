Provenance-grouped protocol for evaluating machine learning in perovskite photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells are typically evaluated using random data splits, which we show systematically leak information through massive internal duplication. Because nearly two-thirds of curated devices share their exact recipe with another, random splits primarily reward the mere recall of historical data rather than testing true physical generalisation. To solve this, we mapped the institutional provenance of the Perovskite Database and established a rigorous hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications, and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models establishes new state-of-the-art performance across all splits and reveals a critical regime shift: while tree ensembles dominate random splits by recognising familiar recipes, their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures excel. Furthermore, feature importance demonstrates that the device architecture and fabrication methodology dictate final efficiency just as strongly as the absorber chemistry. This standardised protocol empowers the community to separate memorisation from true physical discovery.

PING: physics informed graph neural network for advection-diffusion processes with applications to solar power forecasting

Jelena Simeunović, Baptiste Schubnel, Pierre-Jean Alet, Pascal Frossard, Rafael E. Carrillo

Advection-diffusion plays an essential role in transport phenomena such as atmospheric and cloud dynamics. Improved forecasting of cloud motion, in turn, enhances solar power production forecasts and facilitates the integration of additional photovoltaic (PV) sources into the power grid. However, both classical numerical models and emerging deep learning approaches face challenges when applied to spatially irregular sensor networks such as those formed by weather stations. In this work, we adopt a graph signal processing perspective and model advection-diffusion particles as signals on a graph to capture the dynamics of these processes. We introduce a Physics-Informed Graph Neural Network (PING) for forecasting future advection-diffusion particle concentrations on irregular grids. The model incorporates a discretized advection-diffusion equation as a soft constraint in the loss function to guide learning toward physically consistent solutions. This model is on par with state of the art models on synthetic and real transport phenomena datasets, including sea surface temperature and cloud index datasets. Furthermore, the PING model is successfully coupled with models for forecasting PV power production. This hybrid approach leverages data fusion from ground-based sensors at PV stations and satellite images with cloud concentration to improve the accuracy of intra-day PV power production.

Device Model Linking Singlet and Triplet Spin-State Properties to Organic Photovoltaic Device Characteristics

Jun Du, Liming Liu, Jun Yan

This work proposes an exciton-kinetics coupled drift-diffusion (EKDD) model that bridges device-level charge transport with singlet and triplet excited-state kinetics. State populations and reversible photophysical transitions are solved self-consistently, enabling transient and steady-state device response calculations. We investigate how LE-CT, CT-CS, and LE-T energy offsets, together with triplet-transfer kinetics, govern charge generation, recombination, voltage loss, and extraction in organic photovoltaics. We identify CT-CS offset as a primary, largely overlooked factor controlling charge generation and voltage loss. A positive offset makes CT3-CS separation exothermic and improves generation, but lowers CS energy and increases voltage loss; a negative offset makes separation endothermic and highly temperature-sensitive. The model reproduces triplet-mediated recombination in PM6:Y6 and material-dependent triplet recycling in D18:NFA blends. We propose design rules for balanced generation and voltage loss via two charge-transfer channels: the LE-CT-CS pathway favors a moderate LE-CT offset of about 0.15-0.20 eV and a weakly exothermic CT-CS offset of about 0.05-0.075 eV; the T-CT3-CS pathway requires the same CT-CS alignment plus a small CT-T offset, no more than about 0.1 eV, corresponding to a recycling factor K greater than about 10^-2, to maintain fast T-to-CT3 recycling. CT-CS alignment is the main determinant of the charge-generation-voltage-loss trade-off, while triplet-recycling kinetics determines whether this balance survives at the device level. This work provides a unified theoretical description from exciton generation to free charge collection, offering a convenient tool for transient and steady-state analysis in organic photovoltaics.

Identifying these impurities, understanding their impact on device operation, and enabling their rapid detection are essential for upscaling perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, nano-Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy combined with high-resolution optical and scanning probe techniques was used to identify detrimental impurities in wide-bandgap perovskite films relevant to tandem solar cells. Leveraging these insights, a rapid, non-invasive, and high-throughput framework based on high-resolution reflected light microscopy and machine learning-supported image analysis was developed, enabling detection and quantification of harmful impurities in as-prepared films within seconds. Films with higher impurity density show accelerated early degradation, establishing this parameter as an early-warning metric for stability screening. Extending this framework to degradation tracking further reveals coupled photo- and thermo-chemical contributions to impurity-mediated instability. Overall, this work establishes a practical chemically-validated diagnostic imaging framework for rapid pre-screening of perovskite films to enable stable and scalable PSCs.

Engineering Color and Angular Stability in Building-Integrated Photovoltaics Using Metal-Oxide Photonic Glasses

Sinan Genc, Talat Ozden, Alpan Bek

Coloring building-integrated photovoltaics poses a dilemma: absorptive pigments cost photocurrent, while ordered photonic structures are iridescent. Amorphous photonic glasses, disordered packings of high-index nanospheres, are proposed to avoid both, yet their angle-independent color has rarely been quantified on a realistic module stack. A non-absorbing scatterer needs a high index with visible transparency, which the Moss rule caps near 2.35. Coupling Mie scattering, the Percus-Yevick structure factor, the exact phase function, and two-flux transfer with absorption and refraction at the front glass, we fix four target colors that a crowd-sourced naming survey calls unambiguously blue, green, gold, and terracotta, and map the recipes reaching them across four metal oxides, four encapsulants, two cell technologies, and wide ranges of particle size, packing fraction, and layer number. All four are reached in a laminated three-layer coating, to within DeltaE00 of 0.8 to 2.5, but not from one oxide: transparent TiO2 renders the blue and only pale warm tones; the green, gold, and terracotta require the blue-band absorption of V2O5, and the low-contrast oxides turn grey at the warm end. The encapsulant governs the color, and the cell technology does not. Disorder does not confer angle independence: the colors hold for an observer moving under a fixed sun but drift by DeltaE00 of 5 to 18 over the day, terracotta least, and a facade lit from above and viewed from below shifts every color towards the blue. Oxide, layer number, and facade geometry must therefore be designed together.

Divergent Solid-state Conversion Pathways in Evaporated All-perovskite Tandem Solar Cells

Huagui Lai, Amber Wright, Nick Huber, Niels Uythoven, Federico De Giorgi, Jincheng Luo

Sequential thermal evaporation (sTE) is emerging as a solvent-free route to high-quality mid-bandgap perovskites, but its extension to mixed-halide wide-bandgap (WBG) and Sn-Pb narrow-bandgap (NBG) absorbers for all-perovskite tandem solar cells (TSCs) remains limited by an incomplete understanding of solid-state conversion. Here, using time-sliced ex situ analysis, we reveal divergent solid-state conversion mechanisms in sequentially evaporated WBG and NBG precursor stacks. In WBG stacks, formamidinium (FA)-containing species penetrate the inorganic template and Br/I redistribution precedes substantial three-dimensional perovskite formation. The photoactive phase then crystallizes from a chemically mixed reservoir, and absolute PbBr$_2$ thickness, rather than nominal PbBr$_2$/PbI$_2$ ratio, determines the final bandgap. In NBG stacks, by contrast, an early Pb-rich perovskite phase forms upon formamidinium iodide deposition, restricting further FA penetration into the buried SnI$_2$ precursor. Subsequent annealing promotes rapid lattice reorganization faster than Sn/Pb interdiffusion, leaving vertical compositional gradients. Guided by these insights, we develop sTE absorbers with bandgaps spanning 1.26-1.96 eV and demonstrate the first evaporated all-perovskite TSC, reaching a power conversion efficiency of 19.2%. Encapsulated tandems retain on average 80% of their initial efficiency after 1,200 h at 65 $^\circ$C (ISOS-D-2). These results establish bandgap-specific control of solid-state conversion as a design principle for sequentially evaporated perovskite tandem photovoltaics.

Tellurium trioxide as an n-type wide-gap semiconductor with ultrahigh work functions from first principles

Tianwei Wang, Teruya Nagafuji, Kota Hanzawa, Hidenori Hiramatsu, Fumiyasu Oba

The exploration of next-generation wide-gap semiconductors with extreme work functions is highly desirable for the continued advancement of electronic and optoelectronic devices. By using first principles calculations, we show that \b{eta}-TeO3, with a wide band gap exceeding 3 eV, emerges as a promising n-type semiconductor exhibiting ultrahigh work functions of up to ~8 eV. Intentionally or unintentionally incorporated interstitial H, Li, Na, Cu, Mg, and Zn impurities and substitutional F impurities are identified as shallow donors, whereas O vacancies act as minor carrier-compensating defects that locally trap electrons at neighbouring Te sites. The work function is evaluated across a series of low-index nonpolar surfaces. The presence of ordered O vacancies is energetically favoured on these surfaces and tends to reduce the corresponding work functions. These findings underscore the significance of precise control over the O chemical potential to suppress surface O-vacancy formation, thereby preserving the exceptional electronic performance of \b{eta}-TeO3 associated with ultrahigh work functions. Collectively, the results highlight the potential of \b{eta}-TeO3 for hole injection/extraction/transport layers as well as transparent electrodes for applications such as light-emitting devices and photovoltaic cells, while providing useful guidance on carrier doping and surface tuning for these practical applications through elucidating the effects of the point-defect and surface configurations on the electronic properties.

Composition-controlled optical fingerprints and anisotropy of self-trapped excitons in double perovskites

Simone Grillo, Offir Zachs Maishlos, Liya Koren, Rotem Strassberg, Rachel Lifer, Edi Maizenberg

Double perovskites (DPs) offer a compositionally versatile platform for photocatalysis and optoelectronics, but their soft lattices favor strong exciton-phonon coupling and self-trapped excitons (STEs), which produce broad, red-shifted photoluminescence (PL). Here, we combine optical spectroscopy and ab initio calculations to study lead-free, Sb-doped Cs2BInCl6 nanocrystals with B-site alloying from Na to K. Alloying tunes the PL energy from about 2.48 to 2.70 eV, while absorption features near 3.8 eV remain nearly unchanged. Calculations reveal that Sb doping enables bright radiative recombination channels and delocalizes the excitonic wavefunction. We identify STEs responsible for the large PL red shift, associated with an upward shift of dopant-hybridized in-gap states. Na-to-K substitution selectively changes the STE lattice distortion, explaining why PL varies strongly with composition while absorption does not. Theory further predicts pronounced PL polarization anisotropy that reflects the STE wavefunction's spatial structure and depends on B-site composition. Time-resolved anisotropy measurements closely agree with these predictions, providing experimental fingerprints of the excitonic wavefunction and its evolution with alloying. Together, these results disentangle the roles of doping and alloying in lead-free DPs and establish polarization-resolved spectroscopy as a probe of STE wavefunctions.

Theoretical Investigation of Oxygen-Vacancy Diffusion in SrTiO$_3$ using Neural Network Potentials

Kazutaka Nishiguchi, Ryota Yamamoto, Seiichiro Ten-no

Oxygen-vacancy formation and diffusion play important roles in determining the electronic and atomic transport properties of SrTiO$_3$ (STO). Accurate analysis of such point defects requires sufficiently large-scale and long-time molecular dynamics (MD) simulations; however, direct investigations of oxygen-vacancy dynamics using {\it ab initio} MD (AIMD) based on density functional theory (DFT) are limited to relatively small systems and short time scales because of their high computational cost. In this study, we develop a neural network potential (NNP) for pristine and oxygen-deficient STO using a reference dataset generated from AIMD simulations. The trained NNP model accurately reproduces the DFT reference energies, atomic forces, and virials for both pristine STO and STO containing an oxygen vacancy. The NNP also reproduces the supercell-size dependence of the total energies and oxygen-vacancy formation energies, including the formation energy extrapolated to the dilute limit. Large-scale and long-time MD simulations using the NNP (NNP-MD) are then performed to investigate oxygen-vacancy diffusion. The resulting oxygen-vacancy diffusion coefficients show substantially better agreement with experimental data than those obtained from conventional classical MD simulations. An Arrhenius analysis yields an oxygen-vacancy migration enthalpy of 0.64 eV, in close agreement with the experimental value of 0.67 eV. These results demonstrate that NNPs trained on first-principles data provide an accurate and computationally efficient framework for investigating defect formation and long-time vacancy transport in perovskite oxides.

Polarons in Lead-Halide Perovskites: What the Structural Evidence Does and Does Not Establish

Inhee Maeng, Young Mi Lee, Jinwoo Park, Seung Jae Oh, Min-Cherl Jung

Photogenerated carriers in lead-halide perovskites are widely believed to move as large polarons, whose accompanying lattice distortion is thought to screen the carrier from charged defects. Three recent findings call this picture into question. First, time-resolved structural probes now measure the photoinduced lattice distortion directly; yet calculations show that thermal motion of the lattice alone can raise the carrier effective mass-an increase previously taken as evidence for polarons-so that it remains unclear whether the lattice distortion around a carrier is really a polaron. Second, the carrier-lattice coupling strengthens as the material is thinned from 3D to 2D. In the two main families of 2D perovskites, Ruddlesden-Popper and Dion-Jacobson, the carrier couples to the lattice through different channels, and their contrasting behavior is set by which coupling channel dominates rather than by lattice stiffness alone. Third, the first direct test of defect tolerance does not point to polaron screening; its authors attribute defect tolerance to the soft, disordered lattice. New transport data show that polaron screening reduces scattering between carriers, but not that it protects carriers from defects. Polaron formation, even if established, therefore does not establish polarons as the cause of defect tolerance. Separating the two requires measuring the lattice distortion as a function of excitation density, over a range spanning the low densities of a solar cell under sunlight and the much higher densities used in most ultrafast experiments. On this basis, we propose experiments that can clarify how photogenerated carriers behave in lead-halide perovskites.

On Over-Conservatism Under Low-Probability Extreme Events: A KL-Regularized Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Planning Method

Xinda Shen, Yifan Su, Bo Hu, Yanbo Chen, Changzheng Shao, Tao Wu

The increasing penetration of renewable energy makes power systems more vulnerable to low-probability extreme events, elevating system planning to a critical role in ensuring reliability. Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) based on the Wasserstein distance is widely used to address this challenge. However, we find that the conventional Wasserstein ambiguity set tends to excessively amplify the probabilities of low-probability extreme events, leading to over-conservative investment decisions. To mitigate this over-conservatism, this paper proposes a novel ambiguity set that modifies the Wasserstein distance with a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularization term. By leveraging the KL divergence's sensitivity to relative probability changes, the proposed set effectively curbs the unrealistic magnification of low-probability events. A two-stage distributionally robust planning model built upon this new ambiguity set is developed to optimize the installed capacity of photovoltaic, wind, and energy storage. The complex model is then reformulated as an equivalent exponential cone program with strict theoretical proofs, making it computationally tractable. Numerical results on the PJM 5-bus and IEEE 118-bus systems reveal the over-conservatism of standard Wasserstein DRO under low-probability extreme events and validate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed planning model and solution algorithm.

Dynamic Chemical Changes of Ti3C2Tx MXenes in Halide Perovskite Precursor Environments

Kirill Sobolev, Nagaprasad Reddy Samala, Mariela J. Pavan, Sharon Hazan, Vladimir Ezersky, Shakked Schwartz

The integration of two-dimensional Ti3C2Tx MXenes into Halide Perovskite (HaP) optoelectronics has shown immense potential for enhancing device performance, yet the fundamental chemical interactions within the precursor ink (a critical stage determining the final material state) remain largely unexplored. This study presents a comprehensive investigation into the chemical stability and surface evolution of Ti3C2Tx MXenes when exposed to the individual and combined components of a model methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI3) solution. By combining detailed X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) analysis with Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations, we decouple the complex interactions between MXenes and the ink constituents. We demonstrate that elemental iodine (I2) and polyiodides (In-) act as aggressive oxidizers, triggering rapid degradation and dissolution of the MXene flakes. Conversely, lead iodide (PbI2) induces a slower oxidation process driven by the strong adsorption of Pb2+ ions onto oxygen terminated sites.

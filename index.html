Synergistic Graphene and PEDOT:PSS Layers on Silicon for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability in Schottky Junction Solar Cells

Kentaro Nakajo, Hojun Im, Keiichiro Nishimura, Masahiro Teraoka, Masaya Kikuchi

While graphene/n-type silicon (Gr/n-Si) and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si solar cells offer a cost-effective alternative to conventional photovoltaics, achieving simultaneous high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability remains a formidable challenge. Here, we report the synergistic enhancement of both performance and stability in a planar trilayer PEDOT:PSS/Gr/n-Si Schottky junction architecture. By systematically benchmarking this hybrid device against pristine Gr/n-Si and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si reference cells fabricated on untextured substrates, we isolate the fundamental optoelectronic and barrier functions of the carbon lattice. Comprehensive analyses reveal that the PEDOT:PSS layer simultaneously functions as an anti-reflection coating, a moderate p-type dopant for the underlying graphene, and a conductivity enhancer, collectively optimizing interfacial charge transport. Consequently, the trilayer device achieves a PCE of 8.52%, markedly outperforming the pristine Gr/n-Si reference cell (4.56%). Crucially, the atomic impermeability of the graphene sheet serves as a robust environmental barrier. While bare PEDOT:PSS/n-Si junctions retained only ~29% of their initial efficiency after 75 days of ambient exposure, graphene shields the active junction from moisture-induced degradation of the hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS overlayer. This enables the trilayer device to retain approximately 90% of its initial efficiency over the same 75-day period. The complementary roles of graphene as both a highly crystalline charge transport interface and an impermeable protective barrier establish a highly effective strategy for engineering high-efficiency, environmentally stable hybrid photovoltaics.

High-Throughput Imaging of Degradation-Inducing Microscopic Impurities in Perovskite Solar Cells

Sofiia Kosar, Anil R. Pininti, Vladyslav Hnapovskyi, José P. Jurado, Subhashri Mannar, Lorenzo Mardegan

The scalable fabrication of high-quality, large-area perovskite thin films is hindered by microscopic inhomogeneities, particularly residual compositional impurities formed during processing. Identifying these impurities, understanding their impact on device operation, and enabling their rapid detection are essential for upscaling perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, nano-Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy combined with high-resolution optical and scanning probe techniques was used to identify detrimental impurities in wide-bandgap perovskite films relevant to tandem solar cells. Photo-stress experiments revealed that degradation of the perovskite layer initiates at impurity-perovskite interfaces, demonstrating that impurities act as failure nucleation sites. Leveraging these insights, a rapid, non-invasive, and high-throughput framework based on high-resolution reflected light microscopy and machine learning-supported image analysis was developed, enabling detection and quantification of harmful impurities in as-prepared films within seconds. Films with higher impurity density show accelerated early degradation, establishing this parameter as an early-warning metric for stability screening. Extending this framework to degradation tracking further reveals coupled photo- and thermo-chemical contributions to impurity-mediated instability. Overall, this work establishes a practical chemically-validated diagnostic imaging framework for rapid pre-screening of perovskite films to enable stable and scalable PSCs.

Provenance-grouped protocol for evaluating machine learning in perovskite photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells are typically evaluated using random data splits, which we show systematically leak information through massive internal duplication. Because nearly two-thirds of curated devices share their exact recipe with another, random splits primarily reward the mere recall of historical data rather than testing true physical generalisation. To solve this, we mapped the institutional provenance of the Perovskite Database and established a rigorous hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications, and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models establishes new state-of-the-art performance across all splits and reveals a critical regime shift: while tree ensembles dominate random splits by recognising familiar recipes, their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures excel. Furthermore, feature importance demonstrates that the device architecture and fabrication methodology dictate final efficiency just as strongly as the absorber chemistry. This standardised protocol empowers the community to separate memorisation from true physical discovery.

A Hybrid TFT-NBEATS Framework for Robust Multi-Horizon Photovoltaic Power Forecasting

Shafi Bin Sultan, Sabik Bin Sultan, Mohammad Tajrian Arafat Alvi, Rayhanul Islam Sony, Abdulah Al Numan

Accurate multi-horizon photovoltaic (PV) power forecasting is essential for grid stability, energy scheduling, and the efficient operation of PVbatterygrid systems. However, existing models often fail to jointly capture short-term variability and long-term temporal patterns, while lacking unified multi-horizon capability and interpretability. To address these limitations, this study proposes a novel hybrid deep learning framework that integrates a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) and N-BEATS within an attention-based fusion architecture. The proposed model leverages TFT to learn short-term temporal dependencies and dynamic feature importance, while N-BEATS captures long-term trend and seasonal components through interpretable basis decomposition. An adaptive attention mechanism dynamically fuses both branches, enabling balanced multi-scale learning. The framework simultaneously generates forecasts for 30-minute, 2-hour, 6-hour, 12-hour, and 24-hour horizons within a single unified pipeline. The model is evaluated using multi-year real-world datasets from the Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre (DKASC). Experimental results demonstrate superior performance over statistical, machine learning, and deep learning baselines. The proposed model achieves RMSE values of 42.35, 46.27, 52.29, and 48.11 across the 20162017, 2023, 2024, and 20252026 datasets, respectively, corresponding to a 3540% reduction compared to the best baseline (TFT). Additionally, MAPE remains below 5% across all datasets, indicating high predictive accuracy and stability. The model also provides interpretability through attention visualization and trendseasonality decomposition, and is deployed on a cloud-based platform for real-time forecasting. These results demonstrate that the proposed framework is robust, interpretable, and suitable for practical renewable energy applications.

Benchmark for Structured Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Perovskite Photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou, Gaoxiang Yuan

While retrieval-augmented language models increasingly serve as literature assistants in the physical sciences, existing benchmarks evaluate them primarily on qualitative text overlap or through model-based scoring rather than against verified experimental ground truth. In experimental materials disciplines such as perovskite photovoltaics, however, the most critical inquiries are inherently quantitative and aggregate measurements across dozens or hundreds of papers. Here, we present a quantitative question-answering benchmark where all 755 ground-truth answers are computed directly from 45,441 measured device records (40,889 human-curated records through 2021 and 4,552 records extracted from 2022-2026 literature). Benchmarking six retrieval architectures over 158,274 passages from 25,662 papers using an open 14B model on a single 24 GB GPU, standard passage retrieval tops out at 16-23% accuracy (compared to 1.6-6.5% zero-shot). In contrast, equipping the language model with read-only SQL execution over the structured device table elevates accuracy to 51.5% on historical and 50.8% on prospective literature -- a 33.01.8 percentage-point advantage over hybrid text retrieval. Aggregate queries spanning extrema, distributions, and filtered record counts prove virtually unsolvable via text retrieval alone, demonstrating that systematic table extraction is an operational prerequisite for literature-scale quantitative inquiry.

Universal Synthesis of Large-Area Hybrid Perovskite Monolayers Enabling Lithography-Free, Low-Static-Power Optoelectronics

Shankeerthan Kalyanasundaram, Swarit Dwivedi, Sanje Mahasivam, Christopher R. McNeill, Sudha Mokkapati

Solution-phase synthesis of monolayer hybrid organic-inorganic perovskites is constrained by rapid out-of-plane crystal growth, uncontrolled nucleation, and strong interlayer aggregation, yielding flakes of only a few μm. We introduce a steric-engineering ternary-solvent growth strategy to synthesize monolayer and few-layer A2MX4 hybrid perovskites with exceptionally large surface areas. By combining alcohol cosolvents with an amine additive, out-of-plane stacking is suppressed. Using (PEA)2CuCl4 as a model system, monolayers with lateral areas exceeding 1000 μm2 are achieved, representing a massive dimensional increase over conventional methods. Molecular dynamics and quantum-chemical calculations show that longer-chain alcohols reorganize precursor solvation, while solvent volatility prolongs edge-mediated precursor transport during annealing. This strategy demonstrates universal applicability across diverse organic spacer cations (PEA+, F-PEA+, PentA+), metal centers (Cu2+, Mn2+, Pb2+), and halides (Cl-, Br-, I-). The large lateral dimensions enable direct, solvent-free electrical contact fabrication via physical shadow masking, bypassing chemical degradation from solvents used in conventional lithography. Planar devices fabricated from isolated ultrathin (PEA)2PbI4 channels exhibit ultra-low dark currents down to the 10^-15 - 10^-13 A range, enabling femtowatt-to-picowatt static power consumption, ~ 0.2 pW at 5 V, a 250-fold reduction relative to drop-cast films, while maintaining a high light-to-dark current ratio of 4.3 x 102. This work establishes precise steric and solvation control as a paradigm for scalable, compositionally diverse, low-static-power monolayer 2D perovskite optoelectronics.

Vision Transformers for Estimating Irradiance Using Data Scarce Sky Images

David Hamlyn, Sunny Chaudhary, Tasmiat Rahman

Accurate estimation of diffuse horizontal irradiance (DHI) is critical for optimising photovoltaic system performance and energy forecasting yet remains challenging in regions lacking comprehensive ground-based instrumentation. Recent advancements using Vision Transformers (ViTs) trained on extensive sky image datasets have shown promise in replacing costly irradiance measurement equipment, but the scarcity of long-term, high-quality sky imagery significantly restricts practical implementation. Addressing this critical gap, this study proposes a novel dual-framework approach designed for data-scarce scenarios. First, calculated atmospheric parameters, including extraterrestrial irradiance and cyclic time encodings, are integrated to represent sky conditions without utilising any instrumentation. Next, a sequential pipeline initially predicts synthetic global horizontal irradiance (GHI) and uses it as a feature, to refine DHI estimation. Finally, a dual-parallel architecture simultaneously processes raw and overlay-enhanced fisheye sky images. Overlays are generated through unsupervised, physics-informed cloud segmentation to highlight dynamic sky features. Empirical validation is performed using data from the Chilbolton Observatory, chosen for its temperate climate and frequent cloud variability. To simulate data-scarce conditions, models are trained on a single month (e.g., January) and evaluated across a temporally disjoint, full-year test set. Under this setup, the sequential and dual-parallel frameworks achieve RMSE values within 2-3 W/m^2 and 1-6 W/m^2, respectively, of a state-of-the-art ViT trained on the complete dataset. By combining physics-informed modelling with unsupervised segmentation, the proposed method provides a scalable and cost-effective solution for DHI estimation, advancing solar resource assessment in data-constrained environments.

Wavelength-Dependent Photoluminescence Mapping of Depth-Dependent Degradation Under Thermal Stress in Phenethylammonium Tetrafluoroborate Treated Perovskite Solar Cells

Julisa Juarez, Akash Dasgupta, Alan Zhan, Lamtiur P. Samosir, David S. Ginger*

Studying real-time degradation in perovskite semiconductors remains an important challenge. Conventional methods often cannot separate interfacial and bulk processes or rely on destructive analysis. Here, we demonstrate multiwavelength excitation photoluminescence (PL) as a nondestructive approach to distinguish interface- and bulk-dominated recombination losses. Using phenethylammonium tetrafluoroborate (PEABF4) treatment as a model, we use PL to show that PEABF4 passivates the electron transport layer (ETL)/perovskite interface, reducing interfacial recombination losses from fullerene deposition. However, upon thermal aging, PEABF4-treated stacks degrade more rapidly, with recombination losses broadening from the ETL/perovskite interface into the absorber. Diffusion-recombination simulations support interpreting excitation-wavelength-dependent PL decay signatures consistent with changes in surface recombination velocity and bulk lifetime, providing a framework for depth-sensitive comparisons during aging. Collectively, these results further establish multiwavelength excitation PL as a platform for studying recombination losses during aging of perovskite films.

Intensity-driven Mott-like transition in a 2D Ruddlesden-Popper perovskite

Koushik Mondal, Amit Haldar, Indrajit Mondal, Arpita Dutta, Himanshu Bhatt, Biswajit Mahata

Two-dimensional Ruddlesden-Popper-type halide perovskites have emerged as promising cutting-edge nonlinear optical materials due to their strong excitonic effects and carrier-density-dependent optical responses. In this work, we investigate the nonlinear optical properties and ultrafast carrier dynamics of (BA)$_2$PbI$_4$ under infrared excitation, resonant with the two-photon energy of its excitonic transition. Using a nonlinear femtosecond Z-scan measurement with a 1030\,nm pump pulse, we observe a pronounced intensity-dependent evolution of the nonlinear absorption coefficient and the refractive index. With increasing pump intensity, a switch from saturable absorption to reverse saturable absorption emerges, suggesting an intensity-driven Mott-like transition. To elucidate the underlying carrier dynamics, intensity-dependent transient absorption spectroscopy was performed under the same excitation intensities. This reveals a long-lived charge carrier population above the estimated Mott density, reflecting a clear phase transition from an exciton-dominated regime to a dense electron-hole plasma regime. Moreover, such an intensity-driven, high, and switchable nonlinear optical response, along with a Mott-like transition under infrared pulse excitation, makes the 2D (BA)$_2$PbI$_4$ perovskites suitable for all-optical operation in the infrared regime.

Jelena Simeunović, Baptiste Schubnel, Pierre-Jean Alet, Pascal Frossard, Rafael E. Carrillo

Advection-diffusion plays an essential role in transport phenomena such as atmospheric and cloud dynamics. Improved forecasting of cloud motion, in turn, enhances solar power production forecasts and facilitates the integration of additional photovoltaic (PV) sources into the power grid. However, both classical numerical models and emerging deep learning approaches face challenges when applied to spatially irregular sensor networks such as those formed by weather stations. In this work, we adopt a graph signal processing perspective and model advection-diffusion particles as signals on a graph to capture the dynamics of these processes. We introduce a Physics-Informed Graph Neural Network (PING) for forecasting future advection-diffusion particle concentrations on irregular grids. The model incorporates a discretized advection-diffusion equation as a soft constraint in the loss function to guide learning toward physically consistent solutions. This model is on par with state of the art models on synthetic and real transport phenomena datasets, including sea surface temperature and cloud index datasets. Furthermore, the PING model is successfully coupled with models for forecasting PV power production. This hybrid approach leverages data fusion from ground-based sensors at PV stations and satellite images with cloud concentration to improve the accuracy of intra-day PV power production.

Anomalous polar chains in halide perovskites

Erik Fransson, Apinya Ngoipala, Priyanka Deswal, Ethan Berger, Nicholas C. Bristowe, Paul Erhart

Local polar distortions, their collective ordering, and their field-driven reversal have been studied extensively in ferroelectric perovskites for decades. Here we identify an unusual behavior in the ferroelectric halide perovskite CsGeBr3 using large-scale molecular dynamics with first-principles-trained interatomic potentials. In its nominally cubic phase the Ge off-centering forms one-dimensional polar chains that extend over at least tens of nanometers and relax on nanosecond timescales, four orders of magnitude slower than in BaTiO3 and PbTiO3, with CsSnBr3 in between. The cubic CsGeBr3 phase is a dynamically disordered state in which oppositely polarized chains flip throughout the crystal, and a long-lived one-dimensional local polarization coexists with zero net polarization. A simple three-dimensional perovskite thus hosts long-ranged one-dimensional polar correlations with remarkably slow dynamics. In the ferroelectric phase the same chains set a one-dimensional switching mechanism, in which the polarization reverses chain by chain.

Foundation-Style Pretraining for Scalable and Transferable Distributed PV Forecasting

Dayin Chen, Xiaodan Shi, Xin Xu, Zhongyue Su, Shibo Zhu, Ruizhe Deng

The rapid growth of distributed photovoltaic (PV) generation is increasing the demand for accurate forecasting across large numbers of geographically dispersed stations. Existing approaches are commonly developed for individual sites or specific forecasting horizons, requiring repeated training and maintenance as new stations are integrated and limiting direct reuse at previously unseen sites. To address this scalability gap, this paper presents a domain-specific foundation-style pretraining framework that learns a shared forecasting model from heterogeneous multi-site and multi-horizon PV data. NWP-assisted forecasting is formulated as a sequence-completion problem that unifies historical PV power, meteorological observations, future numerical weather prediction (NWP) forecasts, and time encodings. Three pretraining objectives are designed and systematically evaluated, including forecast-aligned reconstruction, generic reconstruction, and mixed reconstruction. The mixed objective combines the operational structure of PV forecasting with random sequence reconstruction and provides the most balanced performance across forecasting accuracy, degraded NWP conditions, and downstream adaptation. Experiments on 479 distributed PV stations use 340 stations for model development and 139 spatially held-out stations for zero-shot evaluation. Across horizons from 6 hours to 7 days, the multi-site pretrained MR model outperforms representative station-specific and shared baselines, as well as generalist time-series models, including a recent large-scale pretrained foundation model. The pretrained model can also be directly deployed at new PV stations and subsequently adapted to region-specific operational objectives when local data become available, providing a scalable forecasting paradigm for large distributed PV fleets.

Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System for extrapolative prediction of perovskite solar cells

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Existing machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells struggle to predict devices made from new materials. Most treat device layers as arbitrary categorical labels and represent perovskite absorbers either through featureless composition vectors or through rigid crystal graphs that require a pre-solved unit cell. As a result, they cannot generalize to unrecorded alloys or unfamiliar cell architectures. We present the Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System (V-PaSS), an architecture built around the physical continuity of perovskite solid solutions. V-PaSS represents arbitrary mixed-cation, mixed-halide absorbers by interpolating relaxed crystal graphs of 23 pure $ABX_3$ endpoint compounds via Vegard's law with learned bandgap bowing. For the device stack, it couples layer sequence and cell polarity (n-i-p or p-i-n) with interfacial band offsets and encodes chemical descriptions with a scientific language model. Evaluated under an institution-grouped protocol where test devices come exclusively from unseen laboratories, an untuned default configuration of V-PaSS achieves the lowest error among fourteen tested models (2.861% MAE, a 5.2% relative reduction over a gradient-boosted baseline at 3.017%, and outperforming the best tuned benchmark at 2.912%). V-PaSS pulls furthest ahead where test devices differ most from training: reducing error by 15.7-19.0% on unseen absorber alloys, by 16.1% on mixed absorbers from new laboratories, and widening its lead over the baseline by 3.6$\times$ on devices with unobserved transport layers. Combined with tree ensembles, V-PaSS sets a new state of the art across all five benchmark splits.

Device Model Linking Singlet and Triplet Spin-State Properties to Organic Photovoltaic Device Characteristics

Jun Du, Liming Liu, Jun Yan

This work proposes an exciton-kinetics coupled drift-diffusion (EKDD) model that bridges device-level charge transport with singlet and triplet excited-state kinetics. State populations and reversible photophysical transitions are solved self-consistently, enabling transient and steady-state device response calculations. We investigate how LE-CT, CT-CS, and LE-T energy offsets, together with triplet-transfer kinetics, govern charge generation, recombination, voltage loss, and extraction in organic photovoltaics. We identify CT-CS offset as a primary, largely overlooked factor controlling charge generation and voltage loss. A positive offset makes CT3-CS separation exothermic and improves generation, but lowers CS energy and increases voltage loss; a negative offset makes separation endothermic and highly temperature-sensitive. The model reproduces triplet-mediated recombination in PM6:Y6 and material-dependent triplet recycling in D18:NFA blends. We propose design rules for balanced generation and voltage loss via two charge-transfer channels: the LE-CT-CS pathway favors a moderate LE-CT offset of about 0.15-0.20 eV and a weakly exothermic CT-CS offset of about 0.05-0.075 eV; the T-CT3-CS pathway requires the same CT-CS alignment plus a small CT-T offset, no more than about 0.1 eV, corresponding to a recycling factor K greater than about 10^-2, to maintain fast T-to-CT3 recycling. CT-CS alignment is the main determinant of the charge-generation-voltage-loss trade-off, while triplet-recycling kinetics determines whether this balance survives at the device level. This work provides a unified theoretical description from exciton generation to free charge collection, offering a convenient tool for transient and steady-state analysis in organic photovoltaics.

Dynamic Chemical Changes of Ti3C2Tx MXenes in Halide Perovskite Precursor Environments

Kirill Sobolev, Nagaprasad Reddy Samala, Mariela J. Pavan, Sharon Hazan, Vladimir Ezersky, Shakked Schwartz

The integration of two-dimensional Ti3C2Tx MXenes into Halide Perovskite (HaP) optoelectronics has shown immense potential for enhancing device performance, yet the fundamental chemical interactions within the precursor ink (a critical stage determining the final material state) remain largely unexplored. This study presents a comprehensive investigation into the chemical stability and surface evolution of Ti3C2Tx MXenes when exposed to the individual and combined components of a model methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI3) solution. By combining detailed X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) analysis with Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations, we decouple the complex interactions between MXenes and the ink constituents. We demonstrate that elemental iodine (I2) and polyiodides (In-) act as aggressive oxidizers, triggering rapid degradation and dissolution of the MXene flakes. Conversely, lead iodide (PbI2) induces a slower oxidation process driven by the strong adsorption of Pb2+ ions onto oxygen terminated sites. In stark contrast, methylammonium iodide (MAI) exhibits a stabilizing effect: it adsorbs onto the MXene surface via hydrogen bonding, effectively passivating the flakes and retarding degradation. In addition, our results reveal that the sequence of precursor addition governs the MXene surface state, requiring extra caution, but also enabling further tunability. These findings provide a critical view on how MXene integration with materials, in general, and HaP in particular, should be perceived, providing a fundamental for their rational engineering.

Domain walls in $R3c$-structured NaNbO$_3$

Jack G. Nedell, Craig J. Fennie

In this work, we undertake a systematic first-principles study of structural domain walls in the low-temperature rhombohedral $R3c$ phase of perovskite oxide NaNbO$_3$. Employing a group-theoretic classification, we introduce eight unique classes of domain walls in the $R3c$ structure, four of which have a domain wall orientation determined uniquely by the conditions of elastic compatibility and charge neutrality, and perform structural relaxations for each representative domain wall class. We find that one class of 109° domain walls has a notably low energy of 9 mJ m$^{-2}$, comparable to the low-energy ferroelectric walls in BaTiO$_3$. Finally, we find that this class of 109° walls is compatible with the crystal twinning observed in experiments on the $R3c$ phase, and propose an atomistic mechanism for the $R3c \leftrightarrow Pbcm$ phase transition.

Electronic Screening as a Design Lever in La-Doped BaTiO$_3$: Coupled Polarization, Dielectric Response, and Optical Anisotropy

Sikandar Azam, Muhammad Fareed, Qaiser Rafiq, Jan Minar, Abed Alataway

Donor substitution can tune the coupled ferroelectric, dielectric, electronic, and optical properties of perovskite oxides by modifying lattice distortions and electronic screening. We investigate these effects in La-doped BaTiO3 using density functional theory, density-functional perturbation theory, and the Berry-phase formalism for polarization. For a model with 12.5% La substitution at the Ba site, La incorporation induces anisotropic lattice contraction, reduces tetragonality and Ti off-centering, and weakens the energetic stabilization of the tetragonal ferroelectric phase. The accompanying reduction in polarization is associated with enhanced electronic screening and reduced anomalous Born effective charges. Phonon calculations indicate that the relaxed doped structure is dynamically stable, while hardening of a low-frequency polar mode is consistent with weakened ferroelectricity. The high-frequency dielectric response increases moderately, whereas the static dielectric response decreases as the lattice contribution is suppressed. Electronic-structure calculations show a Fermi-level shift toward the conduction band without mid-gap defect states. The optical spectra exhibit an absorption-edge shift consistent with a Burstein-Moss-type mechanism, enhanced near-edge transparency, and reduced birefringence. Together, these results suggest a microscopic connection between donor-induced screening, polar-mode hardening, reduced dynamical charges, polarization suppression, and decreased optical anisotropy. The conclusions apply to the investigated 12.5% composition; calculations at additional concentrations are needed to establish quantitative doping trends.

Symmetry-Based Parameter Efficient Low-Rank Fine-Tuning for High-Throughput Calculation of Lattice Thermal Conductivity

Rustam Arabov, Konstantin Sozykin, Nikita Rybin, Alexander Shapeev

Accurate and efficient prediction of lattice thermal conductivity (LTC) is essential for developing materials for thermoelectric, microelectronic, and photovoltaic applications. Here, we present a methodology for high-throughput calculation of LTC across large datasets of compounds sharing a common crystal symmetry. Our approach combines fine-tuning of the MatterSim 1M universal machine-learned interatomic potential with LTC calculations based on the solution of the Boltzmann transport equation. We introduce a symmetry-based few-shot fine-tuning strategy (SBFS-FT) based on low-rank adaptation. In this approach, MatterSim 1M is fine-tuned on a small, carefully selected set of compounds that share a common crystal symmetry. The resulting potential is then applicable for accurate LTC calculations for compounds with this crystal symmetry, even if they were not included in the fine-tuning set. We tested our methodology on datasets of half-Heusler compounds (space group 216), materials with space group 225, and spinels (space group 227). We demonstrated that LTC calculations with MatterSim 1M fine-tuned using SBFS-FT exhibited a mean absolute relative difference from the reference results (obtained using moment tensor potentials and polyMLPs) 2-3 times smaller than that achieved with the non-fine-tuned MatterSim 1M potential. Moreover, the accuracy of LTC calculations with MatterSim 1M after SBFS-FT was comparable to that achieved using the MatterSim 1M potentials individually fine-tuned for each compound. Importantly, SBFS-FT enabled generation of the potential suitable for accurate LTC calculations across large compound datasets substantially faster than fine-tuning of MatterSim 1M for each compound individually, reducing the computational time by approximately a factor of 5-7 for a dataset of 100 compounds.

Identifying these mechanisms-and learning how to select and tune them-is essential for achieving the desired crystal morphology and, ultimately, improving solar-cell performance and stability. To investigate crystallization pathways in evaporating thin films, we develop a simple and general zero-dimensional population-balance model that couples evaporation, chemical reactions, and crystallization. We use the model to explore a range of parameters for a solvent-solute system that follows a two-step crystallization pathway: solvent evaporation first induces formation of a crystalline precursor, which subsequently recrystallizes into the final phase. The model predicts the evolution of the film composition during drying and provides quantitative LaMer curves. Based on these results, we introduce a vocabulary of "supply limitations" and "rate limitations" that enables the interpretation of crystallization kinetics in terms of the governing process conditions.

Climate-Driven Supply-Demand Matching Reshapes Global SolarEV Decarbonization Potential

Yuchen Liu, Stefan Pfenninger-Lee, Tosiyuki Nakaegawa, Takuro Kobashi

Rooftop photovoltaics (PV) can support rapid urban decarbonization, but their contribution is constrained by the imbalance between PV generation and demand, both shaped by local climate. Growing urban electric-vehicle (EV) deployment creates distributed storage near rooftop PV, shifting PV electricity across time. Here we simulate rooftop PV integrated with EVs as batteries across 5,000 grid cells globally, using hourly meteorology and climate-induced demand. We find that PV-EV systems can supply up to 84% of annual building and mobility electricity demand. Self-sufficiency is highest where daytime demand aligns with generation and seasonal variability remains limited. Using PV capacity factor alone to screen PV-EV potential undervalues 21.9% of the world's 1,000 most populous cities because generation-side metrics overlook the temporal coupling of PV generation, electricity demand and EV storage. Substantial PV-EV potential is concentrated in many low-latitude cities, offering a pathway to cleaner urban energy systems while accelerating progress towards carbon neutrality.

Multiwavelength Photoluminescence as a Non-destructive Probe of Depth-Dependent UV Damage in Perovskite Semiconductors

Zixu Huang, Julisa Juarez, Akash Dasgupta, David S. Ginger

Buried interfaces play a critical role in the operational stability of perovskite optoelectronic devices, yet remain challenging to probe non-destructively. Here, we demonstrate a multiwavelength photoluminescence-based approach exploiting the wavelength-dependent absorption of perovskites, enabling fast, and non-destructive probing of depth-sensitive degradation behavior of perovskite devices under operational conditions. Using ultraviolet (UV) light-soaking with different indium tin oxide (ITO) modifiers as a test case, we employ wide-field photoluminescence (PL) imaging, time-resolved photoluminescence (trPL) and ideality factor measurements with different wavelength excitation to characterize degradation behavior of both perovskite thin films and photovoltaic devices under UV soaking. Using only nondestructive characterization, we show that UV-induced degradation in p-i-n perovskite solar cells occurs preferentially at the HTL interface, adversely affecting device stability, consistent with ex-situ destructive tests. Our results also show that modifying the buried interface with 3-mercaptopropyltrimethoxysilane reduces UV-induced damage at the buried interface, thus improving the UV stability of both perovskite films and devices. This work establishes a fast, non-destructive strategy to screen depth-dependent degradation in perovskite devices, offering insights for interface design and stability optimization.

Synergistic Graphene and PEDOT:PSS Layers on Silicon for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability in Schottky Junction Solar Cells

Kentaro Nakajo, Hojun Im, Keiichiro Nishimura, Masahiro Teraoka, Masaya Kikuchi

While graphene/n-type silicon (Gr/n-Si) and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si solar cells offer a cost-effective alternative to conventional photovoltaics, achieving simultaneous high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability remains a formidable challenge. Here, we report the synergistic enhancement of both performance and stability in a planar trilayer PEDOT:PSS/Gr/n-Si Schottky junction architecture. By systematically benchmarking this hybrid device against pristine Gr/n-Si and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si reference cells fabricated on untextured substrates, we isolate the fundamental optoelectronic and barrier functions of the carbon lattice. Comprehensive analyses reveal that the PEDOT:PSS layer simultaneously functions as an anti-reflection coating, a moderate p-type dopant for the underlying graphene, and a conductivity enhancer, collectively optimizing interfacial charge transport. Consequently, the trilayer device achieves a PCE of 8.52%, markedly outperforming the pristine Gr/n-Si reference cell (4.56%). Crucially, the atomic impermeability of the graphene sheet serves as a robust environmental barrier. While bare PEDOT:PSS/n-Si junctions retained only ~29% of their initial efficiency after 75 days of ambient exposure, graphene shields the active junction from moisture-induced degradation of the hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS overlayer. This enables the trilayer device to retain approximately 90% of its initial efficiency over the same 75-day period. The complementary roles of graphene as both a highly crystalline charge transport interface and an impermeable protective barrier establish a highly effective strategy for engineering high-efficiency, environmentally stable hybrid photovoltaics.

Amorphous selenium-alloyed TeOx thin-film solar cells

Zhaoheng Ling, Linqu Luo, Chrysa Aivalioti, Temur Maksudov, Mengxi Zhang, Theodoulos Kalogerakis

Tellurium dioxide (TeO$_2$) is a wide-bandgap oxide with attractive processing versatility and emerging p-type semiconducting behaviour, making it a promising material system for optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications. However, TeO$_2$-derived thin films prepared by scalable manufacturing processes, such as vacuum sublimation, can contain residual metallic Te$^0$ species that introduce trap states, limit hole transport, and promote carrier recombination. Here, we demonstrate a selenium alloying strategy to control the defect chemistry of vacuum-sublimed TeO$_x$ films. TeO$_2$:Se powder mixtures (90:10~wt\%) were thermally evaporated and subsequently annealed in air at 225~$^\circ$C for up to 9~h, yielding amorphous TeO$_x$:Se films with an optical bandgap of 0.98~eV under optimised conditions. Structural, chemical, optical, and electrical analyses show that prolonged annealing promotes Se redistribution, suppresses residual Te$^0$ to approximately 0.4\%, reduces the film's surface roughness, and shifts the valence-band edge. To evaluate the potential of TeO$_x$:Se for photovoltaic applications, we fabricated thin-film solar cells with an ITO/ZnO/TeO$_x$:Se/MoO$_x$/Ag architecture. Devices based on an 80~nm TeO$_x$:Se active layer annealed for 9~h exhibit optimal performance, with broadband photocurrent generation from 300 to 1000~nm, yielding an open-circuit voltage of 0.50~V, a short-circuit current density of 8.50~mA~cm$^{-2}$, a fill factor of 0.44, and a maximum power conversion efficiency of approximately 1.87\%. These results establish selenium-alloyed TeO$_x$ as a viable oxide-derived absorber for thin-film photovoltaics and highlight chemical-state control as an effective route for improving photovoltaic performance.

Electronic-morphological decoupling in mixed-dimensional halide perovskite heterostructures

Mahmoud Samadpour, Alireza Fathi Beiraghvandi, Kazem Meraji, Tristan Sachsenweger Ballantyne, Fuxiang Ji, Mahdi Mohammadi

Surface morphology is widely considered an indicator of thin-film quality, with smoother films generally expected to exhibit more uniform optoelectronic properties. Here, we demonstrate that substantial surface evolution does not necessarily lead to increased electronic heterogeneity in mixed-dimensional perovskite heterostructures. We investigate how low-dimensional Ruddlesden-Popper capping layers derived from the sterically hindered 5-tert-butyl-phenanthridinium cation affect the spatial distributions of photoluminescence (PL) characteristics and quasi-Fermi level splitting (QFLS) in 3D perovskites. By combining statistical atomic force microscopy (AFM) analysis with confocal, spatially resolved PL spectroscopy, we correlate surface topography with microscale optoelectronic properties. Although capping increases surface roughness and height asymmetry, it does not increase electronic inhomogeneity. Instead, the heterostructures exhibit enhanced PL, reduced non-radiative recombination, and improved spatial uniformity of radiative QFLS compared with pristine 3D films. Under accelerated aging, this contrast becomes more pronounced: pristine films develop substantial spatial fluctuations in emission and QFLS, whereas capped heterostructures largely preserve their optoelectronic homogeneity. These findings indicate that the low-dimensional capping layer suppresses aging-induced optoelectronic disorder, demonstrating that electronic uniformity can be maintained despite morphological irregularity. This decoupling provides a design principle for developing durable, high-performance perovskite photovoltaics.

Provenance-grouped protocol for evaluating machine learning in perovskite photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells are typically evaluated using random data splits, which we show systematically leak information through massive internal duplication. Because nearly two-thirds of curated devices share their exact recipe with another, random splits primarily reward the mere recall of historical data rather than testing true physical generalisation. To solve this, we mapped the institutional provenance of the Perovskite Database and established a rigorous hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications, and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models establishes new state-of-the-art performance across all splits and reveals a critical regime shift: while tree ensembles dominate random splits by recognising familiar recipes, their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures excel. Furthermore, feature importance demonstrates that the device architecture and fabrication methodology dictate final efficiency just as strongly as the absorber chemistry. This standardised protocol empowers the community to separate memorisation from true physical discovery.

Controlling Surface Chemistry in Tin-Lead Perovskites for High-Efficiency Solar Cells

Lijun Chen, Sitong Zhao, Gaukhar Nigmetova, David Garcia Romero, Matteo Pitaro, Sofia Lerda

Interfacial nonradiative recombination limits tin-lead perovskite photovoltaics. Here we show that neutral ethylenediamine chelates undercoordinated metals at tin-rich surfaces, weakens metal-iodide bonds, and removes excess tin, reconstructing the surface towards a stoichiometric tin-to-lead ratio. In contrast, protonated ethylenediammonium iodide initially passivates undercoordinated metal sites before undergoing defect-assisted deprotonation. Atomistic simulations reveal that subsurface iodide vacancies promote iodide migration and lower the energetic cost of deprotonation. Ethylenediamine-induced reconstruction produces stronger n-type surface character, facilitates electron extraction into C60, and suppresses interfacial nonradiative recombination, enabling methylammonium-free solar cells with a power conversion efficiency of 25.86%. Together, these distinct surface reaction pathways and the defect-assisted evolution of the passivating species underscore the importance of understanding perovskite surface chemistry for the rational design of molecular passivation strategies.

The Utility of Physics-Informed Machine Learning in Solar Power Production Forecasting

Peter Pavlík, Franz M. Rohrhofer, Bernhard C. Geiger

Physics-informed machine learning (PIML) and hybrid modeling have attracted significant attention as approaches for combining physical knowledge with the flexibility of deep learning. In this work, we investigate whether these approaches deliver their commonly claimed benefits for large-scale photovoltaic (PV) power forecasting. We develop two hybrid forecasting architectures that integrate the ADR physical PV efficiency model with the TSMixer neural forecasting architecture through feature augmentation and residual learning. The proposed models are evaluated on a dataset comprising over 3 million samples from 3,713 PV installations across 33 European countries. We assess convergence speed, forecasting accuracy, transferability across geographical regions, and data efficiency. Although the physics-informed models seem to exhibit slightly faster convergence during training, they do not achieve consistent improvements in test-set accuracy, generalization, or performance under limited training data compared to the purely data-driven baseline. These findings suggest that, for modern deep forecasting architectures trained on large datasets, explicitly incorporating the ADR physical model provides limited practical benefit for day-ahead PV forecasting.

IMPACT: Inverter-Informed Multimodal Learning for Photovoltaic Generation Forecasting

Anushka Katiyar, Cong Feng, Jing Wang, Tristan Liu, Simon Julien, Zachary Jacobs

This work develops a physics-informed multimodal deep learning framework for 10- to 110-minute-ahead probabilistic photovoltaic power forecasting. The forecasting model is built on a four-branch architecture that separately encodes plant-level temporal history and inverter-level spatial cloud dynamics through a graph attention encoder with a learned adjacency, known-future solar geometry, and the current meteorological state, fused through gated cross-attention. The geometry branch adds two forward-looking physical signals, spatial cloud heterogeneity and inverter ramp-down activity, each empirically weighted toward the horizon where it is most predictive. The decoder anchors its prediction to a smart persistence baseline, learning a horizon-conditioned correction, and the resulting quantile forecasts are calibrated online through a kernel-weighted adaptive conformal calibration step. The proposed model outperforms all 14 baselines, achieving a forecast skill score (FSS) of 0.2950, 0.099 above the next-best model. A branch-composition ablation shows that every branch contributes positively and the model retains substantial skill in challenging overcast and variable sky conditions (FSS 0.198 and 0.255).

Sophie Tucker, Isaac Metcalf, Rachel C. Kilbride, Elena J. Cassella, Jessica H. Persaud, Faiz Mandani

Ammonium thiocyanate (NH4SCN) has been used extensively to improve the current extraction and device performance of quasi-two-dimensional perovskite solar cells. Whilst this additive has been shown to influence quasi-2D perovskite crystallisation, a detailed understanding of its mechanism has not yet been established. Through spectroscopic and structural studies, we demonstrate that NH4SCN enables quasi-2D perovskite phases to structurally template the dominant 3D phase. Contrary to previous reports, we suggest that the benefit of this additive comes from an improvement to the 3D perovskite structure, not from charges transferring through vertically oriented 2D perovskite layers. We show that this additive provides kinetic control over the quasi-2D phase formation, suppressing the unfavourable n = 1 perovskite phase and promoting the growth of high-n 2D scaffold phases by extending the nucleation window. This promotes the in-situ templated growth of MAPbI3. We provide robust structural analysis demonstrating highly (110)-ordered 3D perovskite, with the mechanism confirmed by close lattice matching between this phase and quasi-2D phases. Furthermore, we show that this structural control is cation dependent, requiring both close lattice matching between quasi-2D and 3D perovskite phases, and successful kinetic control of the relevant quasi-2D phases.

VerifiedLab: Harnessing Knowns for Unknowns

Yu Yao, Jiayi Dong, Yang Yang, Shuiwang Ji, Ju Li

Across human history, knowledge has undergone several transitions, from orality to writing and then to digital archives. At each transition, humans established new conventions to preserve knowledge for the new era. Nowadays, as research agents are increasingly deployed for laboratory experiments, knowledge is crossing another boundary: from records written for humans to those for machines. In this work, we introduce VerifiedLab, a multi-agent harness framework for this human-to-machine transition of knowledge. VerifiedLab transforms research output from passive artifacts into active systems that accelerate downstream use, adoption and prediction of future trends. In VerifiedLab, a team of AI scientists with distinct roles reasons over records verified field by field: a principal investigator plans, a librarian computes the evidence directly from the records, specialists draft, and a gate and a critic check each claim against the measured reliability of its data before the harness decides whether to release or withhold it. We demonstrate the efficacy of VerifiedLab through extensive experiments on perovskite solar cells: VerifiedLab recovers known physical trends, anticipates devices reported a year later, and lets AI scientists inherit the literature together with a measure of how far it can be trusted. By scaling the inference-time verification compute, VerifiedLab introduces a new pathway for knowledge dissemination and machine harness for AI co-scientists.

AutoEnergyDesign: An LLM-Driven Agent for Automatic Energy System Design Optimization

Amedeo Buonanno, Salvatore Fabozzi, Maria Valenti

Designing building energy systems is a high-dimensional, mixed-integer search problem: when each design is evaluated by a year-long simulation, black-box optimizers typically need hundreds of evaluations. This paper presents AutoEnergyDesign, a framework in which a Large Language Model (LLM) agent reasons over previous simulation results, proposes configuration changes and learns in context from each outcome, while a git-based mechanism automatically reverts unsuccessful changes. The agent couples a reasoning model with an explicit memory organisation (procedural, semantic, episodic and reflective), a constrained action interface and an environment-grounded keep/revert signal. The simulator covers eight technologies (photovoltaics, micro-wind, combined heat and power, solar thermal, gas boilers, batteries, heat pumps and inverters) and computes the 25-year Net Present Cost (NPC), including capital and operating costs and salvage value. On a case study with measured building load profiles, the agent, run with Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 (ten runs of 50 simulations each), is compared with random search, Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) Bayesian optimization and a $(1+1)$ evolution strategy sharing the agent's acceptance rule (300 evaluations, ten seeds). With all three models the agent reaches an NPC of about 3.53 MEUR, 1.20% below the best TPE result and 0.23% below the best evolution-strategy result. It comes within 1% of this design after a median of 18 (Opus) to 27.5 (Haiku) simulations, whereas among the baselines only the evolution strategy does so, in half of its seeds and after a median of 192 evaluations. Opus reaches the best design in all ten runs, Sonnet in six (two runs never come within 1%), and Haiku comes within 1% in every run but reaches it exactly only once. Every decision is recorded in an auditable trace.

Mahesh Eledath-Changarath, Jesús Ortiga-Fibla, Andrés F. Gualdrón-Reyes, Jesús Rodríguez-Romero, Iván Mora-Seró, Ana Cros

In these materials, the choice of the organic spacer plays a key role not only in enhancing environmental stability, but also in determining their structural characteristics and carrier dynamics. Our findings indicate that structural and chemical engineering, along with surface passivation and encapsulation strategies, are essential for achieving long-term stability in two-dimensional halide perovskite devices. Nevertheless, optimizing the balance between stability and device performance remains a critical consideration.

Confinement Controlled Phase-Pure 2D Layered Perovskites for Band-Edge Engineering

Danila A. Tatarinov, Alexander Schleusener, Roman Krahne

Controlling the number n of connected octahedra layers in two-dimensional metal halide perovskites enables fine control of the semiconductor band gap that is highly advantageous for optoelectronic and light emission applications. Phase purity in terms of n is crucial towards narrow emission bands and the design of band structure alignment for charge carrier funneling. We present a simple and scalable synthesis method using cesium and butylammonium as A-cations to fabricate microcrystal powders of 2D lead bromide perovskites that results in samples with very high n-phase purity. The obtained microcrystals show a sharp onset in absorbance and single narrow emission peak in UV-blue spectral range, and we confirm their structure and composition with X-ray diffraction and energy dispersive spectroscopy. We show that we can tailor the spectral absorption of microcrystals samples by controlled mixing of the n-phase pure powders, and we demonstrate this capability with spectral photocurrent measurements using pellets pressed from pure and mixed powders. Both the spectral and dynamic response of the photocurrent of the pellets depend strongly on the composition of the powders, highlighting the design potential of our approach towards band-edge engineering.

Accurate multi-horizon photovoltaic (PV) power forecasting is essential for grid stability, energy scheduling, and the efficient operation of PVbatterygrid systems. However, existing models often fail to jointly capture short-term variability and long-term temporal patterns, while lacking unified multi-horizon capability and interpretability. To address these limitations, this study proposes a novel hybrid deep learning framework that integrates a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) and N-BEATS within an attention-based fusion architecture. The proposed model leverages TFT to learn short-term temporal dependencies and dynamic feature importance, while N-BEATS captures long-term trend and seasonal components through interpretable basis decomposition. An adaptive attention mechanism dynamically fuses both branches, enabling balanced multi-scale learning. The framework simultaneously generates forecasts for 30-minute, 2-hour, 6-hour, 12-hour, and 24-hour horizons within a single unified pipeline. The model is evaluated using multi-year real-world datasets from the Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre (DKASC). Experimental results demonstrate superior performance over statistical, machine learning, and deep learning baselines. The proposed model achieves RMSE values of 42.35, 46.27, 52.29, and 48.11 across the 20162017, 2023, 2024, and 20252026 datasets, respectively, corresponding to a 3540% reduction compared to the best baseline (TFT). Additionally, MAPE remains below 5% across all datasets, indicating high predictive accuracy and stability. The model also provides interpretability through attention visualization and trendseasonality decomposition, and is deployed on a cloud-based platform for real-time forecasting. These results demonstrate that the proposed framework is robust, interpretable, and suitable for practical renewable energy applications.

Polarization-locked chiral persistent spin textures in two-dimensional lead-halide perovskites

Palanichamy Gayathri, Biplab Sanyal, Jonas Fransson

Chiral spacer cations crystallize two-dimensional hybrid perovskites in the polar Sohncke group $P2_1$, which places the polar axis in the inorganic layer. We analyze the spin-orbit field that this geometry allows and test it against first-principles spin textures of ten polar chiral lead-halide perovskites, their mirror enantiomers and four halide-substituted analogues. The point group $C_2$ with the twofold axis in the layer forbids the helical Rashba field and allows a linear term $γ_1 k_x σ_z$, the Rashba interaction of the in-plane polar axis, together with chirality-related Dresselhaus and radial terms. Thirty-four two-band $k \cdot p$ models were fitted to each band pair and ranked by the agreement of their spin directions with the calculation rather than by the band energies, which cannot distinguish them. In all strongly polar compounds, the conduction band is described by the persistent term alone, with $γ_1 = 1.09$-$1.98$ eV Å and a spin agreement of at least 0.98, whereas the best Rashba or Dresselhaus model reaches 0.20. The $R$ and $S$ enantiomers of each compound have opposite polarizations, and therefore opposite $γ_1$ and opposite persistent spin textures of equal magnitude, as required by the mirror that relates them; this holds in all ten compounds, so the choice of the enantiomer selects the spin direction. The magnitude of $γ_1$ correlates with the disparity of the in-plane Pb-X-Pb angles ($r = 0.95$). With electron masses of $0.26$-$0.43\,m_e$ along the spin-splitting directions, the associated persistent spin helices have wavelengths of 3.4-8.2 nm.

Unifying Low-Frequency Raman and Infrared Signatures across Perovskites

Ethan Berger, Erik Fransson, Apinya Ngoipala, Lucas Svensson, Priyanka Deswal, Mehmet Baskurt

Perovskites have been studied for a wide range of applications, from oxide-based ferroelectrics to halide-perovskite photovoltaics. Despite their diverse functionalities, these materials share strong lattice anharmonicity arising from structural instabilities such as polar off-centering and octahedral tilting. These anharmonic distortions strongly influence the low-frequency vibrational response and can be observed in infrared (IR) and Raman spectra. However, low-frequency IR and Raman signatures are typically investigated separately, hindering the development of a unified understanding of vibrational spectroscopy across perovskite classes. In this work, we use machine-learning-based molecular dynamics to simulate the IR and Raman spectra of oxide perovskites (SrTiO$_3$ and BaTiO$_3$), halide perovskites (CsPbBr$_3$, CsSnBr$_3$, and CsGeBr$_3$) and double halide perovskites (Cs$_2$AgAlBr$_6$ and Cs$_2$AgBiBr$_6$). By comparing materials exhibiting polar, tilt-driven and mixed instabilities, we find that polar off-centering leads to enhanced low-frequency IR and dielectric responses, whereas nonpolar modes, particularly octahedral tilting, are associated with Raman central peaks. The strength of the low-frequency features also depends on how strongly the underlying modes couple to polarization or dielectric susceptibility. Finally, we show that the dynamics of these instabilities play an important role, with slow polar reorientations shifting the dielectric response to very low frequencies and overdamped tilting modes producing persistent Raman central peaks. These results establish a unified framework connecting low-frequency IR and Raman spectra to the symmetry, coupling and dynamics of their underlying anharmonic lattice instabilities.

Military installation energy readiness depends on having sufficient resources to sustain mission-critical loads throughout a prolonged utility outage. A robust viability kernel identifies combinations of battery energy, fuel, and load-shifting capability sufficient to sustain critical load under uncertain demand and photovoltaic generation. A probability bound translates stochastic generator start failures and recovery into a deterministic generator count selected to meet a specified availability requirement at islanding. Case studies for a fourteen-day islanding requirement at 0.999 islanding-entry availability show that advance warning of an outage can be used to prepare resources to meet a higher readiness requirement before islanding begins. These results demonstrate how active resource management can preserve long-duration energy readiness. The framework also helps commanders understand how readiness changes with operating conditions, even when the available resources remain the same.

Which wind and solar modelling choices matter? Effects on capacity factors, energy droughts and European power system configuration

L. Karkossa, A. Grochowicz, M. J. Koivisto, A. Hahmann, M. VIctoria

Europe's future power system will rely heavily on wind and solar power and must remain resilient during prolonged periods of low wind and solar generation, known as variable renewable energy (VRE) droughts. To capture such rare events, adequacy assessments increasingly draw on decades of weather data, but the conversion of weather into power is largely treated as fixed. It remains unclear which modelling choices in this conversion matter for representing these droughts, and whether they matter as much as the weather itself. Here we systematically compare these choices side by side (bias correction, wind speed extrapolation, capacity layouts, turbine and photovoltaic configurations, and wake losses) and trace their effects through VRE droughts to the design of a net-zero European power system, using 85 years of reanalysis data. We find that modelling choices barely change how often VRE droughts occur or when the system is stressed. They do, however, determine how much capacity is needed to withstand that stress. By altering the electricity delivered during droughts, they shift required capacity by up to 480 GW, which is comparable to the spread across the most critical weather years. Modelling exercises should therefore use multiple weather years; bias-correct and regionally validate weather inputs; explicitly represent resource heterogeneity, forward-looking technologies, and wake losses; and judge these choices by their effect on the low-generation tail, not only on the mean.

Hierarchical interfacial anchoring for durable perovskite solar cells

Jiaxin Wu, Hongzhuo Wu, Zuhong Zhang, Xiaoyu Guan, Jinbo Zhao, Yuting Guo

Although inverted perovskite solar cells now deliver certified efficiencies approaching their single-junction limits, their practical deployment remains constrained by the thermo-mechanical fragility of buried interfaces under realistic temperature fluctuations. Here, we identify thermo-mechanically driven, stress-accumulation-dominated adhesion failure at self-assembled monolayer-based buried interfaces as a critical failure mode in inverted perovskite solar cells. To address this issue, we propose an interfacial robustness design strategy based on a homometallic halide pretreatment, which constructs a chemically compatible and highly anchorable interfacial environment on transparent conductive oxides to guide the ordered assembly of SAMs. This interfacial treatment increases the density of surface hydroxyl species and enables hierarchical multipoint interfacial anchoring dominated by metal-oxygen coordination, with Cl- induced hydrogen-bonding interactions acting cooperatively, thereby enhancing anchoring robustness and improving tolerance to thermo-mechanical stress accumulation. With this interfacial strategy, the devices achieve a power conversion efficiency of 27.3% (26.9% certified) and retain 94% of their initial efficiency after 400 temperature cycles between -40 and 85 degrees C (ISOS-T-3). These results demonstrate that rational regulation of buried interfacial anchoring architectures can simultaneously enhance device efficiency and long-term operational reliability, offering a viable pathway toward high-efficiency and durable perovskite photovoltaics.

Synergistic Graphene and PEDOT:PSS Layers on Silicon for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability in Schottky Junction Solar Cells

Kentaro Nakajo, Hojun Im, Keiichiro Nishimura, Masahiro Teraoka, Masaya Kikuchi

While graphene/n-type silicon (Gr/n-Si) and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si solar cells offer a cost-effective alternative to conventional photovoltaics, achieving simultaneous high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability remains a formidable challenge. Here, we report the synergistic enhancement of both performance and stability in a planar trilayer PEDOT:PSS/Gr/n-Si Schottky junction architecture. By systematically benchmarking this hybrid device against pristine Gr/n-Si and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si reference cells fabricated on untextured substrates, we isolate the fundamental optoelectronic and barrier functions of the carbon lattice. Comprehensive analyses reveal that the PEDOT:PSS layer simultaneously functions as an anti-reflection coating, a moderate p-type dopant for the underlying graphene, and a conductivity enhancer, collectively optimizing interfacial charge transport. Consequently, the trilayer device achieves a PCE of 8.52%, markedly outperforming the pristine Gr/n-Si reference cell (4.56%). Crucially, the atomic impermeability of the graphene sheet serves as a robust environmental barrier. While bare PEDOT:PSS/n-Si junctions retained only ~29% of their initial efficiency after 75 days of ambient exposure, graphene shields the active junction from moisture-induced degradation of the hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS overlayer. This enables the trilayer device to retain approximately 90% of its initial efficiency over the same 75-day period. The complementary roles of graphene as both a highly crystalline charge transport interface and an impermeable protective barrier establish a highly effective strategy for engineering high-efficiency, environmentally stable hybrid photovoltaics.

Dynamic Molecular Transformation Mediated Crystallization Regulation and Interface Passivation in Tin Based Perovskite Solar Cells

Ying Tang, Zuhong Zhang, Hongzhuo Wu, Zhihao Li, Zhenhuang Su, Yonggang Yang

Tin-based perovskites are promising candidates for lead-free photovoltaics but suffer from labile Sn$^{2+}$ coordination, rapid crystallization, and severe interfacial defects. Here, we develop a dynamic molecular transformation using 1-acetylpiperazine (PX) to regulate crystallization and interface chemistry simultaneously. During nucleation and growth, the carbonyl group coordinates with Sn$^{2+}$ to stabilize precursor complexes, moderate supersaturation, and enable controlled crystal growth. Upon annealing, PX undergoes in situ molecular transformation, suggested to form amidinium-functionalized species that preferentially occupy surface A-site vacancies, forming a passivation layer and suppressing nonradiative recombination. This coupled bulk-to-interface evolution yields compact, highly crystalline films with improved optoelectronic quality. Devices achieve a power conversion efficiency of 15.18% and retain 94.1% of initial efficiency after 500 h of continuous illumination. This work demonstrates a dynamic molecular engineering approach integrating crystallization control and interface passivation, providing a pathway toward efficient and stable lead-free perovskite photovoltaics.

Evidence-grounded large language models guide perovskite solar cell design and optimization

YanKai Chen, Zhi Wan, ZiHao Li, Tao Jing

Using large language models to interpret experimental evidence, design discriminating experiments and formulate well-supported mechanistic explanations remains a central challenge in materials research. Here we establish PSM-Bench, an 800-question benchmark developed by perovskite researchers to evaluate scientific reasoning in perovskite photovoltaics. We also introduce OpenPerov, an expert-informed language-model framework that learns from expert-calibrated evidence records reconstructed from 4,035 training articles; these records link experimental strategies, controls and observations to mechanistic interpretations. Structured-evidence adaptation outperformed conventional question-answer training, and OpenPerov Flash ranked first among 13 closed-book systems. OpenPerov Pro raised Scientific Mastery further, from 87.6 to 89.1, by revising selected passages with retrieved evidence. On questions derived from subsequent studies, both models also outscored expert-authored answers. In an experimental test of self-assembled monolayer design, OpenPerov selected a K analogue that outperformed the Na analogue preferred by experts. Devices incorporating the selected contact reached a power conversion efficiency of 27.05% and retained 96.74% of their initial efficiency after 1,000 h of dark thermal ageing at 85 $^\circ$C. These results show that reconstructed scientific reasoning provides an effective basis for building domain models that interpret experiments and inform materials design.

Multiwavelength Photoluminescence as a Non-destructive Probe of Depth-Dependent UV Damage in Perovskite Semiconductors

Zixu Huang, Julisa Juarez, Akash Dasgupta, David S. Ginger

Buried interfaces play a critical role in the operational stability of perovskite optoelectronic devices, yet remain challenging to probe non-destructively. Here, we demonstrate a multiwavelength photoluminescence-based approach exploiting the wavelength-dependent absorption of perovskites, enabling fast, and non-destructive probing of depth-sensitive degradation behavior of perovskite devices under operational conditions. Using ultraviolet (UV) light-soaking with different indium tin oxide (ITO) modifiers as a test case, we employ wide-field photoluminescence (PL) imaging, time-resolved photoluminescence (trPL) and ideality factor measurements with different wavelength excitation to characterize degradation behavior of both perovskite thin films and photovoltaic devices under UV soaking. Using only nondestructive characterization, we show that UV-induced degradation in p-i-n perovskite solar cells occurs preferentially at the HTL interface, adversely affecting device stability, consistent with ex-situ destructive tests. Our results also show that modifying the buried interface with 3-mercaptopropyltrimethoxysilane reduces UV-induced damage at the buried interface, thus improving the UV stability of both perovskite films and devices. This work establishes a fast, non-destructive strategy to screen depth-dependent degradation in perovskite devices, offering insights for interface design and stability optimization.

Controlling Surface Chemistry in Tin-Lead Perovskites for High-Efficiency Solar Cells

Lijun Chen, Sitong Zhao, Gaukhar Nigmetova, David Garcia Romero, Matteo Pitaro, Sofia Lerda

Interfacial nonradiative recombination limits tin-lead perovskite photovoltaics. Here we show that neutral ethylenediamine chelates undercoordinated metals at tin-rich surfaces, weakens metal-iodide bonds, and removes excess tin, reconstructing the surface towards a stoichiometric tin-to-lead ratio. In contrast, protonated ethylenediammonium iodide initially passivates undercoordinated metal sites before undergoing defect-assisted deprotonation. Atomistic simulations reveal that subsurface iodide vacancies promote iodide migration and lower the energetic cost of deprotonation. Ethylenediamine-induced reconstruction produces stronger n-type surface character, facilitates electron extraction into C60, and suppresses interfacial nonradiative recombination, enabling methylammonium-free solar cells with a power conversion efficiency of 25.86%. Together, these distinct surface reaction pathways and the defect-assisted evolution of the passivating species underscore the importance of understanding perovskite surface chemistry for the rational design of molecular passivation strategies.

Analytical Framework Relating Capacitance-Frequency Measurements and Hysteresis in Perovskite Solar Cells

Manjeet Kumar, Changzeng Ding, Mathias Nyman, Yongqi Sun, Chang-Qi Ma, Ronald Österbacka

Mobile ion migration strongly influences the electrical response and current-voltage hysteresis of perovskite solar cells. However, quantitative determination of ionic transport parameters typically requires transient measurements, extensive numerical simulations, and/or fitting procedures involving multiple uncertain parameters. Here, we present an analytical model that establishes a direct relationship between capacitance-frequency (C-f) characteristics and ionic transport properties in perovskite solar cells, while accounting for interlayer effects. The model describes the transition from the ion-screened depletion capacitance at low frequencies to the geometric capacitance at high frequencies and demonstrates that the characteristic transition frequency provides a direct measure of the dielectric relaxation time for ions. Furthermore, the model establishes a quantitative connection between C-f characteristics and scan-rate-dependent current-voltage hysteresis, enabling prediction of the scan-rate window in which maximum hysteresis occurs. Drift-diffusion simulations show excellent agreement with the analytical predictions over wide ranges of ion density, ion mobility, transport-layer mobility and doping concentration. Experimental measurements confirm the predicted correlation between capacitance and hysteresis. By providing a direct, physically transparent, and computationally efficient route for extracting ionic transport parameters from routine impedance measurements, this work offers a practical tool for quantifying ion migration and understanding dynamic phenomena in perovskite solar cells.

MOTIF: LLM-Defined Material Taxonomies for OOD Property Prediction via Invariant Feature Selection

Gwangseon Jang, Sieun Yun, Dong Ho Lee, Kyong-Ha Lee, Chanuk Lim, Jin Woo Choi

Out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization remains a central challenge in materials property prediction, where models must extrapolate to novel chemistries beyond the training distribution. While large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong scientific reasoning, their direct use for quantitative prediction remains limited. To address this, we systematically evaluate multiple roles for LLM assistance, finding that only taxonomy-based grouping consistently improves OOD generalization over a strong kNN+GBT baseline. Building on this, we here propose MOTIF, a framework that leverages LLMs not as predictors, but as generators of candidate taxonomies that partition materials into chemically meaningful environments. These taxonomies are evaluated via training-only pseudo-OOD validation, and only those that improve generalization are retained. Invariant feature selection is then applied within each accepted taxonomy, followed by gated aggregation across taxonomies. We evaluate on 2D halide perovskites, where bandgap prediction must generalize across distinct organic spacer families. On this benchmark, MOTIF reduces mean OOD MAE from 0.122 to 0.111 eV. Under a frozen dev/test protocol, the deployable configuration further improves errors from 0.123 to 0.117 eV (electronic bandgap) and from 0.181 to 0.173 eV (optical bandgap). Beyond this primary task, we validate transferability across additional materials datasets, including 3D perovskite solar cell efficiencies and Matbench benchmarks with structure-based splits. Across domains, MOTIF consistently improves robustness under distribution shift, outperforming expert-defined groupings, rule-based heuristics, and clustering baselines, while remaining competitive with strong classical selection methods. These results demonstrate that LLMs are most effective not as predictors, but as tools for discovering structure in data.

Amorphous selenium-alloyed TeOx thin-film solar cells

Zhaoheng Ling, Linqu Luo, Chrysa Aivalioti, Temur Maksudov, Mengxi Zhang, Theodoulos Kalogerakis

Tellurium dioxide (TeO$_2$) is a wide-bandgap oxide with attractive processing versatility and emerging p-type semiconducting behaviour, making it a promising material system for optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications. However, TeO$_2$-derived thin films prepared by scalable manufacturing processes, such as vacuum sublimation, can contain residual metallic Te$^0$ species that introduce trap states, limit hole transport, and promote carrier recombination. Here, we demonstrate a selenium alloying strategy to control the defect chemistry of vacuum-sublimed TeO$_x$ films. TeO$_2$:Se powder mixtures (90:10~wt\%) were thermally evaporated and subsequently annealed in air at 225~$^\circ$C for up to 9~h, yielding amorphous TeO$_x$:Se films with an optical bandgap of 0.98~eV under optimised conditions. Structural, chemical, optical, and electrical analyses show that prolonged annealing promotes Se redistribution, suppresses residual Te$^0$ to approximately 0.4\%, reduces the film's surface roughness, and shifts the valence-band edge. To evaluate the potential of TeO$_x$:Se for photovoltaic applications, we fabricated thin-film solar cells with an ITO/ZnO/TeO$_x$:Se/MoO$_x$/Ag architecture. Devices based on an 80~nm TeO$_x$:Se active layer annealed for 9~h exhibit optimal performance, with broadband photocurrent generation from 300 to 1000~nm, yielding an open-circuit voltage of 0.50~V, a short-circuit current density of 8.50~mA~cm$^{-2}$, a fill factor of 0.44, and a maximum power conversion efficiency of approximately 1.87\%. These results establish selenium-alloyed TeO$_x$ as a viable oxide-derived absorber for thin-film photovoltaics and highlight chemical-state control as an effective route for improving photovoltaic performance.

Strain-dependent Dynamics of Interactions of Iodine-vacancy pairs in CsPbI3: A DFT and Machine-Learning Molecular Dynamics Study

Donggeun Kang, Seongju Lee, Yonggoon Jeon

Iodine-vacancy migration and association are important factors in the structural stability of halide perovskites. Although neighboring vacancies have been suggested to form energetically favorable pairs, their stability and reconfiguration under finite-temperature lattice deformation remain unclear. Here, we combine machine-learning molecular dynamics (MLMD) and density functional theory (DFT) to investigate neighboring iodine vacancies in tilted CsPbI3 under isotropic and biaxial strain. The MLMD trajectories reveal a nonmonotonic dependence of short-time hopping fractions on strain, with strong isotropic compression favoring point-like separation and tension promoting pair-preserving relay hopping. Notably, biaxial compression produces distinct orientation-dependent responses: vacancy pairs initially aligned with the compressed plane preferentially undergo separation, whereas those perpendicular to the plane predominantly exhibit relay hopping. Complementary static calculations using DFT and the SevenNet-0 potential yield repulsive interactions for all configurations examined. These findings highlight the importance of finite-temperature dynamics and strain-dependent local geometry in understanding vacancy-pair stability and reconfiguration in halide perovskites.

Yet their adoption across material platforms is constrained by lithography, multistep pattern transfer, persistent masks, and material-specific etching. Here, we introduce a lithography-free two-step strategy in which thermally evaporated self-assembled Ga nanoparticles act as self-consuming masks during Ar inductively coupled plasma reactive-ion etching. During nanostructuring, the sun-weighted reflectance reaches minima of 11-15% on the monocrystalline substrates, whereas the perovskite achieves 0.5% after complete mask consumption. The approach therefore combines a self-assembled mask, its consumption, and pattern transfer in a single physical etching step, providing a simple route to light-coupling surfaces for III-V, Si, and perovskite optoelectronics.

Inverse design of superconductors and semiconductors with differentiable foundation models

Cristóbal Méndez, Justin Tahmassebpur, Tomás A. Arias

Designing a crystal for a target property requires choosing its elements, stoichiometry, and atomic structure together, yet gradients exist only for the continuous structural variables. We introduce differentiable Equiformer fast effective-atom theory (DEFEAT), which lets a pretrained EquiformerV3 foundation model accept continuous species weights and site occupancies without retraining on fractional data, thereby enabling gradient-based materials optimization. Gradients from fine-tuned property models and a machine-learned energy penalty then guide updates to composition, atom count, atomic positions, and lattice vectors in a single optimization. In a search for phonon-mediated superconductors, density-functional perturbation theory on 45 structurally novel designs gives isotropic Allen-Dynes critical temperatures above 10 K for 39; the highest is 24.0 K for ZrTiNb2CN3, and H4LiPd3 (23.2 K) lies on the static DFT hull. In searches for photovoltaic absorbers, SCAN calculations confirm ten novel crystal structures with band gaps within 20% of a 1.34 eV target on their respective raw or empirically corrected scales, all within 70 meV/atom of the DFT hull, and show that the systematic gap error of the training functional propagates into the designs unless the objective corrects for it. Quasiharmonic free energies from machine-learned potentials further show that finite-temperature competition with solid phases can reorder candidates relative to the static hull.

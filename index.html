Evidence-grounded large language models guide perovskite solar cell design and optimization

YanKai Chen, Zhi Wan, ZiHao Li, Tao Jing

Using large language models to interpret experimental evidence, design discriminating experiments and formulate well-supported mechanistic explanations remains a central challenge in materials research. Here we establish PSM-Bench, an 800-question benchmark developed by perovskite researchers to evaluate scientific reasoning in perovskite photovoltaics. We also introduce OpenPerov, an expert-informed language-model framework that learns from expert-calibrated evidence records reconstructed from 4,035 training articles; these records link experimental strategies, controls and observations to mechanistic interpretations. Structured-evidence adaptation outperformed conventional question-answer training, and OpenPerov Flash ranked first among 13 closed-book systems. OpenPerov Pro raised Scientific Mastery further, from 87.6 to 89.1, by revising selected passages with retrieved evidence. On questions derived from subsequent studies, both models also outscored expert-authored answers. In an experimental test of self-assembled monolayer design, OpenPerov selected a K analogue that outperformed the Na analogue preferred by experts. Devices incorporating the selected contact reached a power conversion efficiency of 27.05% and retained 96.74% of their initial efficiency after 1,000 h of dark thermal ageing at 85 $^\circ$C. These results show that reconstructed scientific reasoning provides an effective basis for building domain models that interpret experiments and inform materials design.

Multiwavelength Photoluminescence as a Non-destructive Probe of Depth-Dependent UV Damage in Perovskite Semiconductors

Zixu Huang, Julisa Juarez, Akash Dasgupta, David S. Ginger

Buried interfaces play a critical role in the operational stability of perovskite optoelectronic devices, yet remain challenging to probe non-destructively. Here, we demonstrate a multiwavelength photoluminescence-based approach exploiting the wavelength-dependent absorption of perovskites, enabling fast, and non-destructive probing of depth-sensitive degradation behavior of perovskite devices under operational conditions. Using ultraviolet (UV) light-soaking with different indium tin oxide (ITO) modifiers as a test case, we employ wide-field photoluminescence (PL) imaging, time-resolved photoluminescence (trPL) and ideality factor measurements with different wavelength excitation to characterize degradation behavior of both perovskite thin films and photovoltaic devices under UV soaking. Using only nondestructive characterization, we show that UV-induced degradation in p-i-n perovskite solar cells occurs preferentially at the HTL interface, adversely affecting device stability, consistent with ex-situ destructive tests. Our results also show that modifying the buried interface with 3-mercaptopropyltrimethoxysilane reduces UV-induced damage at the buried interface, thus improving the UV stability of both perovskite films and devices. This work establishes a fast, non-destructive strategy to screen depth-dependent degradation in perovskite devices, offering insights for interface design and stability optimization.

Enrichment-Oriented Virtual Screening of Perovskite Solar Cells through Reinforcement Learning

Yezeyuan Zheng, Ziyang Hu, Maojun Sun. Ruijian Han, Jian Huang, Guanhua Chen, Jie Liu

Experimental optimization of perovskite solar cells is constrained by combinatorial diversity in absorber compositions, device layers, and processing, which makes virtual screening attractive for prioritizing recipes before fabrication. We develop a surrogate-guided reinforcement-learning framework that casts recipe generation as virtual screening, enriching candidate shortlists near high-performing experimental neighbors. A frozen Tabular Prior-data Fitted Network (TabPFN) guides recipe generation, while a reference pool of 890 measured devices enables neighborhood-based assessment. Five-fold evaluation on 1,846 devices shows top-ten selection raises mean power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 19.68\% to 23.74\%. Three learned policies are compared with six baselines under 3,000 surrogate evaluations per run. In top-100 shortlists, Proximal policy optimization (PPO) and recurrent PPO (RPPO) achieve neighborhood-supported hit rates of 23\% and 24\% (7.9- and 8.3-fold Pure Random's 2.9\%), versus 10\% (3.4-fold) for the baselines with the highest supported hit rate (L-SHADE and Ax BO, tied) at $K = 100$. Gains coexist with weak-support fractions. Of 9,000 recipes, 65.4\% match a composition family at similarity 0.80, falling to 11.7\% at 0.90. Results support enrichment-oriented prioritization but not experimental performance, seed robustness, or matched-budget advantage.

Hierarchical interfacial anchoring for durable perovskite solar cells

Jiaxin Wu, Hongzhuo Wu, Zuhong Zhang, Xiaoyu Guan, Jinbo Zhao, Yuting Guo

Although inverted perovskite solar cells now deliver certified efficiencies approaching their single-junction limits, their practical deployment remains constrained by the thermo-mechanical fragility of buried interfaces under realistic temperature fluctuations. Here, we identify thermo-mechanically driven, stress-accumulation-dominated adhesion failure at self-assembled monolayer-based buried interfaces as a critical failure mode in inverted perovskite solar cells. To address this issue, we propose an interfacial robustness design strategy based on a homometallic halide pretreatment, which constructs a chemically compatible and highly anchorable interfacial environment on transparent conductive oxides to guide the ordered assembly of SAMs. This interfacial treatment increases the density of surface hydroxyl species and enables hierarchical multipoint interfacial anchoring dominated by metal-oxygen coordination, with Cl- induced hydrogen-bonding interactions acting cooperatively, thereby enhancing anchoring robustness and improving tolerance to thermo-mechanical stress accumulation. With this interfacial strategy, the devices achieve a power conversion efficiency of 27.3% (26.9% certified) and retain 94% of their initial efficiency after 400 temperature cycles between -40 and 85 degrees C (ISOS-T-3). These results demonstrate that rational regulation of buried interfacial anchoring architectures can simultaneously enhance device efficiency and long-term operational reliability, offering a viable pathway toward high-efficiency and durable perovskite photovoltaics.

Dynamic Molecular Transformation Mediated Crystallization Regulation and Interface Passivation in Tin Based Perovskite Solar Cells

Ying Tang, Zuhong Zhang, Hongzhuo Wu, Zhihao Li, Zhenhuang Su, Yonggang Yang

Tin-based perovskites are promising candidates for lead-free photovoltaics but suffer from labile Sn$^{2+}$ coordination, rapid crystallization, and severe interfacial defects. Here, we develop a dynamic molecular transformation using 1-acetylpiperazine (PX) to regulate crystallization and interface chemistry simultaneously. During nucleation and growth, the carbonyl group coordinates with Sn$^{2+}$ to stabilize precursor complexes, moderate supersaturation, and enable controlled crystal growth. Upon annealing, PX undergoes in situ molecular transformation, suggested to form amidinium-functionalized species that preferentially occupy surface A-site vacancies, forming a passivation layer and suppressing nonradiative recombination. This coupled bulk-to-interface evolution yields compact, highly crystalline films with improved optoelectronic quality. Devices achieve a power conversion efficiency of 15.18% and retain 94.1% of initial efficiency after 500 h of continuous illumination. This work demonstrates a dynamic molecular engineering approach integrating crystallization control and interface passivation, providing a pathway toward efficient and stable lead-free perovskite photovoltaics.

Controlling Surface Chemistry in Tin-Lead Perovskites for High-Efficiency Solar Cells

Lijun Chen, Sitong Zhao, Gaukhar Nigmetova, David Garcia Romero, Matteo Pitaro, Sofia Lerda

Interfacial nonradiative recombination limits tin-lead perovskite photovoltaics. Here we show that neutral ethylenediamine chelates undercoordinated metals at tin-rich surfaces, weakens metal-iodide bonds, and removes excess tin, reconstructing the surface towards a stoichiometric tin-to-lead ratio. In contrast, protonated ethylenediammonium iodide initially passivates undercoordinated metal sites before undergoing defect-assisted deprotonation. Atomistic simulations reveal that subsurface iodide vacancies promote iodide migration and lower the energetic cost of deprotonation. Ethylenediamine-induced reconstruction produces stronger n-type surface character, facilitates electron extraction into C60, and suppresses interfacial nonradiative recombination, enabling methylammonium-free solar cells with a power conversion efficiency of 25.86%. Together, these distinct surface reaction pathways and the defect-assisted evolution of the passivating species underscore the importance of understanding perovskite surface chemistry for the rational design of molecular passivation strategies.

How to Effectively Manage the Energy in Residential Systems: Development and Comparison of Methods

Julian Ruddick

The transition from fossil fuels presents significant challenges in energy management. To ensure a sustainable energy future, it is crucial to reduce energy consumption when possible and to synchronize electricity consumption and storage with the production from sources with low greenhouse gas emissions. This thesis explores a variety of energy management methods used to manage batteries, heat pumps, and other energy-related systems. The thesis presents three simulation cases alongside one real-world experiment to compare energy management done through simple rules, optimization, and machine learning methods. Furthermore, this thesis introduces TreeC, a novel machine learning-based method that learns understandable yet efficient energy management strategies, applicable to many cases. The three key contributions of this thesis can be summarized as follows: 1) The developed TreeC method demonstrates competitive performance compared to a variety of energy management approaches, such as model predictive control and reinforcement learning, across a diverse set of energy management problems. The understandability of TreeC's energy management strategies greatly improves the transparency and trustworthiness of machine learning-based energy management. 2) The real-world experiment highlights the need to validate energy management methods on actual hardware and not solely on simulations. Both TreeC and model predictive control performed worse than in simulations, due to, respectively, insufficiently representative training data and the real-time input data not being reliable enough. 3) Energy management algorithms reduce costs in electrified Belgian dwellings, particularly in poorly insulated dwellings, electric vehicle chargers, and photovoltaic installations. However, on average, these cost savings do not yet outweigh the current costs and effort required to implement such algorithms.

Amorphous selenium-alloyed TeOx thin-film solar cells

Zhaoheng Ling, Linqu Luo, Chrysa Aivalioti, Temur Maksudov, Mengxi Zhang, Theodoulos Kalogerakis

Tellurium dioxide (TeO$_2$) is a wide-bandgap oxide with attractive processing versatility and emerging p-type semiconducting behaviour, making it a promising material system for optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications. However, TeO$_2$-derived thin films prepared by scalable manufacturing processes, such as vacuum sublimation, can contain residual metallic Te$^0$ species that introduce trap states, limit hole transport, and promote carrier recombination. Here, we demonstrate a selenium alloying strategy to control the defect chemistry of vacuum-sublimed TeO$_x$ films. TeO$_2$:Se powder mixtures (90:10~wt\%) were thermally evaporated and subsequently annealed in air at 225~$^\circ$C for up to 9~h, yielding amorphous TeO$_x$:Se films with an optical bandgap of 0.98~eV under optimised conditions. Structural, chemical, optical, and electrical analyses show that prolonged annealing promotes Se redistribution, suppresses residual Te$^0$ to approximately 0.4\%, reduces the film's surface roughness, and shifts the valence-band edge. To evaluate the potential of TeO$_x$:Se for photovoltaic applications, we fabricated thin-film solar cells with an ITO/ZnO/TeO$_x$:Se/MoO$_x$/Ag architecture. Devices based on an 80~nm TeO$_x$:Se active layer annealed for 9~h exhibit optimal performance, with broadband photocurrent generation from 300 to 1000~nm, yielding an open-circuit voltage of 0.50~V, a short-circuit current density of 8.50~mA~cm$^{-2}$, a fill factor of 0.44, and a maximum power conversion efficiency of approximately 1.87\%. These results establish selenium-alloyed TeO$_x$ as a viable oxide-derived absorber for thin-film photovoltaics and highlight chemical-state control as an effective route for improving photovoltaic performance.

Interpretable machine learning diagnosis of degradation mechanisms in lead-free $RbSn_{0.5}Ge_{0.5}I_{3}$ solar cells from temperature- and illumination-dependent electrical measurements

Nabanita Sarker, Hafiz Imtiaz

Machine learning coupled with device simulation is widely used to optimize thin-film solar cells, but almost exclusively in the forward direction, from device parameters to predicted performance. The inverse problem encountered after fabrication, namely identifying the mechanism that limits a given device, is still addressed by manual inspection of characterization data. In this work, both directions are examined for lead-free $RbSn_{0.5}Ge_{0.5}I_{3}$ solar cells using approximately 42,000 SCAPS-1D simulations. Replacing the Spiro-OMeTAD hole transport layer with CuI increases the simulated power conversion efficiency from 19.6\% to 21.6\%, almost entirely through the fill factor. Surrogate models trained and tested on a factorial parameter grid substantially overstate their predictive accuracy. An XGBoost regressor attains $R^2 = 0.9997$ on held-out grid points but only 0.58 on independently sampled devices, whereas training on a Latin hypercube design of comparable size restores $R^2 = 0.995$. For the inverse problem, a multi-label classifier identifies which of six degradation mechanisms are present from simulated temperature- and illumination-dependent open-circuit-voltage measurements. It achieves a macro $F_1$ score of 0.938, compared to 0.687 for a calibrated physics-rule baseline. The classifier does not, however, generalize to combinations of mechanisms absent from the training set.

Explainable AI for Solar PV Power Forecasting: From Model Selection to Trustworthy, Reproducible Deployment

Nuzhat Khan, Muhammad Paend Bakht, Ab Al-Hadi Ab Rahman, Fabiano Pallonetto, Amy Fahy, Mohamed Khalafalla Hassan

Machine learning (ML) is now a standard tool for forecasting the output of solar photovoltaic (PV) plants, yet published accuracy figures are often hard to compare and harder to trust. Many studies validate with random splits, report no reference forecast, and present feature attributions without saying what they can and cannot show. This paper reviews the main families of ML methods for PV power forecasting and resource assessment, from tree ensembles to deep and foundation models, and sets out a reproducible workflow that combines automated model search with explainable AI. The workflow is then tested on an openly available dataset: a 16.32 kWp plant in Porto, Portugal, forecast one day ahead from a 4 km numerical weather prediction grid. Over a full test year, the best models reach a normalised root mean square error (nRMSE) of 11.6% of installed capacity, about half the error of smart persistence. The choice of input features changed accuracy far more than the choice among strong algorithms, and shuffled cross-validation was mildly optimistic. After conformal calibration, the nominal 80% prediction intervals covered 81.7% of observations, against 64.5% before. SHAP rankings stayed stable when the model was retrained on different periods but shifted when correlated inputs were removed, so attributions should be read as properties of a model rather than of the plant. The paper closes with practical lessons and open problems, including deployment on edge hardware and the effect of a changing climate.

Electronic-morphological decoupling in mixed-dimensional halide perovskite heterostructures

Mahmoud Samadpour, Alireza Fathi Beiraghvandi, Kazem Meraji, Tristan Sachsenweger Ballantyne, Fuxiang Ji, Mahdi Mohammadi

Surface morphology is widely considered an indicator of thin-film quality, with smoother films generally expected to exhibit more uniform optoelectronic properties. Here, we demonstrate that substantial surface evolution does not necessarily lead to increased electronic heterogeneity in mixed-dimensional perovskite heterostructures. We investigate how low-dimensional Ruddlesden-Popper capping layers derived from the sterically hindered 5-tert-butyl-phenanthridinium cation affect the spatial distributions of photoluminescence (PL) characteristics and quasi-Fermi level splitting (QFLS) in 3D perovskites. By combining statistical atomic force microscopy (AFM) analysis with confocal, spatially resolved PL spectroscopy, we correlate surface topography with microscale optoelectronic properties. Although capping increases surface roughness and height asymmetry, it does not increase electronic inhomogeneity. Instead, the heterostructures exhibit enhanced PL, reduced non-radiative recombination, and improved spatial uniformity of radiative QFLS compared with pristine 3D films. Under accelerated aging, this contrast becomes more pronounced: pristine films develop substantial spatial fluctuations in emission and QFLS, whereas capped heterostructures largely preserve their optoelectronic homogeneity. These findings indicate that the low-dimensional capping layer suppresses aging-induced optoelectronic disorder, demonstrating that electronic uniformity can be maintained despite morphological irregularity. This decoupling provides a design principle for developing durable, high-performance perovskite photovoltaics.

Impact of organic spacer cations on the light and thermal stability of two-dimensional lead halide perovskites

Mahesh Eledath-Changarath, Jesús Ortiga-Fibla, Andrés F. Gualdrón-Reyes, Jesús Rodríguez-Romero, Iván Mora-Seró, Ana Cros

Long-term stability remains a critical challenge limiting the photovoltaic and optoelectronic applications of two-dimensional lead halide perovskites. In these materials, the choice of the organic spacer plays a key role not only in enhancing environmental stability, but also in determining their structural characteristics and carrier dynamics. Here, we present an environmental photo- and thermal-stability analysis of BA2PbI4 and PEA2PbI4 two-dimensional lead halide perovskites by means of confocal micro-Raman and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy techniques. We have investigated two different organic spacers, n-butylammonium (BA) and 2-phenylethylammonium (PEA), which provide comparable room-temperature luminescence in freshly exfoliated perovskite nanoflakes and degrade similarly through the removal of organic components and fast crystal phase decomposition into PbI2 and Pb0. Our findings indicate that structural and chemical engineering, along with surface passivation and encapsulation strategies, are essential for achieving long-term stability in two-dimensional halide perovskite devices. Nevertheless, optimizing the balance between stability and device performance remains a critical consideration.

AgBiS2 near-infrared absorbers for thin film tandem photovoltaics

Shiling Dong, Yongjie Wang, Hae-Jun Seok, Mengyuan Huang, Edward Newton, Der Ying Chen

However, fulfilling this potential requires overcoming the limited transport properties of AgBiS2 nanocrystal films, which constrains film thicknesses to only 30-50 nm, severely restricting NIR response. A solution-phase ligand exchange process is herein developed, allowing one-step deposition of passivated AgBiS2 nanocrystals that maintain efficient charge-carrier extraction despite an order of magnitude increase in film thickness. These films enable 24.0%-efficient four-terminal tandems with visible-light-absorbing lead-halide perovskite sub-cells, and monolithic tandems successfully demonstrating voltage addition. AgBiS2 PVs have high stability, retaining >90% of the original efficiency after 3 months of storage in dry air without encapsulation. This work establishes the significant potential of AgBiS2 as a stable and scalable NIR absorber for tandem PVs.

The response is therefore determined by the position of the halide-p bands relative to the valence band maximum, not by the atomic spin-orbit constant, a criterion that applies to any semiconductor with an orbital-singlet band edge. These results point to CsPbI3 as a candidate gate-controlled source of spin current for perovskite spin-orbit torque devices.

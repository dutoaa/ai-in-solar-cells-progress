Synergistic Graphene and PEDOT:PSS Layers on Silicon for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability in Schottky Junction Solar Cells

Kentaro Nakajo, Hojun Im, Keiichiro Nishimura, Masahiro Teraoka, Masaya Kikuchi

While graphene/n-type silicon (Gr/n-Si) and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si solar cells offer a cost-effective alternative to conventional photovoltaics, achieving simultaneous high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability remains a formidable challenge. Here, we report the synergistic enhancement of both performance and stability in a planar trilayer PEDOT:PSS/Gr/n-Si Schottky junction architecture. By systematically benchmarking this hybrid device against pristine Gr/n-Si and PEDOT:PSS/n-Si reference cells fabricated on untextured substrates, we isolate the fundamental optoelectronic and barrier functions of the carbon lattice. Comprehensive analyses reveal that the PEDOT:PSS layer simultaneously functions as an anti-reflection coating, a moderate p-type dopant for the underlying graphene, and a conductivity enhancer, collectively optimizing interfacial charge transport. Consequently, the trilayer device achieves a PCE of 8.52%, markedly outperforming the pristine Gr/n-Si reference cell (4.56%). Crucially, the atomic impermeability of the graphene sheet serves as a robust environmental barrier. While bare PEDOT:PSS/n-Si junctions retained only ~29% of their initial efficiency after 75 days of ambient exposure, graphene shields the active junction from moisture-induced degradation of the hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS overlayer. This enables the trilayer device to retain approximately 90% of its initial efficiency over the same 75-day period. The complementary roles of graphene as both a highly crystalline charge transport interface and an impermeable protective barrier establish a highly effective strategy for engineering high-efficiency, environmentally stable hybrid photovoltaics.

High-Throughput Imaging of Degradation-Inducing Microscopic Impurities in Perovskite Solar Cells

Sofiia Kosar, Anil R. Pininti, Vladyslav Hnapovskyi, José P. Jurado, Subhashri Mannar, Lorenzo Mardegan

The scalable fabrication of high-quality, large-area perovskite thin films is hindered by microscopic inhomogeneities, particularly residual compositional impurities formed during processing. Identifying these impurities, understanding their impact on device operation, and enabling their rapid detection are essential for upscaling perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, nano-Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy combined with high-resolution optical and scanning probe techniques was used to identify detrimental impurities in wide-bandgap perovskite films relevant to tandem solar cells. Photo-stress experiments revealed that degradation of the perovskite layer initiates at impurity-perovskite interfaces, demonstrating that impurities act as failure nucleation sites. Leveraging these insights, a rapid, non-invasive, and high-throughput framework based on high-resolution reflected light microscopy and machine learning-supported image analysis was developed, enabling detection and quantification of harmful impurities in as-prepared films within seconds. Films with higher impurity density show accelerated early degradation, establishing this parameter as an early-warning metric for stability screening. Extending this framework to degradation tracking further reveals coupled photo- and thermo-chemical contributions to impurity-mediated instability. Overall, this work establishes a practical chemically-validated diagnostic imaging framework for rapid pre-screening of perovskite films to enable stable and scalable PSCs.

Provenance-grouped protocol for evaluating machine learning in perovskite photovoltaics

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells are typically evaluated using random data splits, which we show systematically leak information through massive internal duplication. Because nearly two-thirds of curated devices share their exact recipe with another, random splits primarily reward the mere recall of historical data rather than testing true physical generalisation. To solve this, we mapped the institutional provenance of the Perovskite Database and established a rigorous hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications, and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models establishes new state-of-the-art performance across all splits and reveals a critical regime shift: while tree ensembles dominate random splits by recognising familiar recipes, their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures excel. Furthermore, feature importance demonstrates that the device architecture and fabrication methodology dictate final efficiency just as strongly as the absorber chemistry. This standardised protocol empowers the community to separate memorisation from true physical discovery.

Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System for extrapolative prediction of perovskite solar cells

Qiwei Han, Chi Zhou

Existing machine-learning models for perovskite solar cells struggle to predict devices made from new materials. Most treat device layers as arbitrary categorical labels and represent perovskite absorbers either through featureless composition vectors or through rigid crystal graphs that require a pre-solved unit cell. As a result, they cannot generalize to unrecorded alloys or unfamiliar cell architectures. We present the Virtual-Alloy Physics-Aware Semantic System (V-PaSS), an architecture built around the physical continuity of perovskite solid solutions. V-PaSS represents arbitrary mixed-cation, mixed-halide absorbers by interpolating relaxed crystal graphs of 23 pure $ABX_3$ endpoint compounds via Vegard's law with learned bandgap bowing. For the device stack, it couples layer sequence and cell polarity (n-i-p or p-i-n) with interfacial band offsets and encodes chemical descriptions with a scientific language model. Evaluated under an institution-grouped protocol where test devices come exclusively from unseen laboratories, an untuned default configuration of V-PaSS achieves the lowest error among fourteen tested models (2.861% MAE, a 5.2% relative reduction over a gradient-boosted baseline at 3.017%, and outperforming the best tuned benchmark at 2.912%). V-PaSS pulls furthest ahead where test devices differ most from training: reducing error by 15.7-19.0% on unseen absorber alloys, by 16.1% on mixed absorbers from new laboratories, and widening its lead over the baseline by 3.6$\times$ on devices with unobserved transport layers. Combined with tree ensembles, V-PaSS sets a new state of the art across all five benchmark splits.

Divergent Solid-state Conversion Pathways in Evaporated All-perovskite Tandem Solar Cells

Huagui Lai, Amber Wright, Nick Huber, Niels Uythoven, Federico De Giorgi, Jincheng Luo

Sequential thermal evaporation (sTE) is emerging as a solvent-free route to high-quality mid-bandgap perovskites, but its extension to mixed-halide wide-bandgap (WBG) and Sn-Pb narrow-bandgap (NBG) absorbers for all-perovskite tandem solar cells (TSCs) remains limited by an incomplete understanding of solid-state conversion. Here, using time-sliced ex situ analysis, we reveal divergent solid-state conversion mechanisms in sequentially evaporated WBG and NBG precursor stacks. In WBG stacks, formamidinium (FA)-containing species penetrate the inorganic template and Br/I redistribution precedes substantial three-dimensional perovskite formation. The photoactive phase then crystallizes from a chemically mixed reservoir, and absolute PbBr$_2$ thickness, rather than nominal PbBr$_2$/PbI$_2$ ratio, determines the final bandgap. In NBG stacks, by contrast, an early Pb-rich perovskite phase forms upon formamidinium iodide deposition, restricting further FA penetration into the buried SnI$_2$ precursor. Subsequent annealing promotes rapid lattice reorganization faster than Sn/Pb interdiffusion, leaving vertical compositional gradients. Guided by these insights, we develop sTE absorbers with bandgaps spanning 1.26-1.96 eV and demonstrate the first evaporated all-perovskite TSC, reaching a power conversion efficiency of 19.2%. Encapsulated tandems retain on average 80% of their initial efficiency after 1,200 h at 65 $^\circ$C (ISOS-D-2). These results establish bandgap-specific control of solid-state conversion as a design principle for sequentially evaporated perovskite tandem photovoltaics.

Jelena Simeunović, Baptiste Schubnel, Pierre-Jean Alet, Pascal Frossard, Rafael E. Carrillo

Advection-diffusion plays an essential role in transport phenomena such as atmospheric and cloud dynamics. Improved forecasting of cloud motion, in turn, enhances solar power production forecasts and facilitates the integration of additional photovoltaic (PV) sources into the power grid. However, both classical numerical models and emerging deep learning approaches face challenges when applied to spatially irregular sensor networks such as those formed by weather stations. In this work, we adopt a graph signal processing perspective and model advection-diffusion particles as signals on a graph to capture the dynamics of these processes. We introduce a Physics-Informed Graph Neural Network (PING) for forecasting future advection-diffusion particle concentrations on irregular grids. This model is on par with state of the art models on synthetic and real transport phenomena datasets, including sea surface temperature and cloud index datasets. Furthermore, the PING model is successfully coupled with models for forecasting PV power production. This hybrid approach leverages data fusion from ground-based sensors at PV stations and satellite images with cloud concentration to improve the accuracy of intra-day PV power production.

Device Model Linking Singlet and Triplet Spin-State Properties to Organic Photovoltaic Device Characteristics

Jun Du, Liming Liu, Jun Yan

This work proposes an exciton-kinetics coupled drift-diffusion (EKDD) model that bridges device-level charge transport with singlet and triplet excited-state kinetics. State populations and reversible photophysical transitions are solved self-consistently, enabling transient and steady-state device response calculations. We investigate how LE-CT, CT-CS, and LE-T energy offsets, together with triplet-transfer kinetics, govern charge generation, recombination, voltage loss, and extraction in organic photovoltaics. We identify CT-CS offset as a primary, largely overlooked factor controlling charge generation and voltage loss. A positive offset makes CT3-CS separation exothermic and improves generation, but lowers CS energy and increases voltage loss; a negative offset makes separation endothermic and highly temperature-sensitive. The model reproduces triplet-mediated recombination in PM6:Y6 and material-dependent triplet recycling in D18:NFA blends. We propose design rules for balanced generation and voltage loss via two charge-transfer channels: the LE-CT-CS pathway favors a moderate LE-CT offset of about 0.15-0.20 eV and a weakly exothermic CT-CS offset of about 0.05-0.075 eV; the T-CT3-CS pathway requires the same CT-CS alignment plus a small CT-T offset, no more than about 0.1 eV, corresponding to a recycling factor K greater than about 10^-2, to maintain fast T-to-CT3 recycling. CT-CS alignment is the main determinant of the charge-generation-voltage-loss trade-off, while triplet-recycling kinetics determines whether this balance survives at the device level. This work provides a unified theoretical description from exciton generation to free charge collection, offering a convenient tool for transient and steady-state analysis in organic photovoltaics.

Engineering Color and Angular Stability in Building-Integrated Photovoltaics Using Metal-Oxide Photonic Glasses

Sinan Genc, Talat Ozden, Alpan Bek

Coloring building-integrated photovoltaics poses a dilemma: absorptive pigments cost photocurrent, while ordered photonic structures are iridescent. Amorphous photonic glasses, disordered packings of high-index nanospheres, are proposed to avoid both, yet their angle-independent color has rarely been quantified on a realistic module stack. A non-absorbing scatterer needs a high index with visible transparency, which the Moss rule caps near 2.35. Coupling Mie scattering, the Percus-Yevick structure factor, the exact phase function, and two-flux transfer with absorption and refraction at the front glass, we fix four target colors that a crowd-sourced naming survey calls unambiguously blue, green, gold, and terracotta, and map the recipes reaching them across four metal oxides, four encapsulants, two cell technologies, and wide ranges of particle size, packing fraction, and layer number. All four are reached in a laminated three-layer coating, to within DeltaE00 of 0.8 to 2.5, but not from one oxide: transparent TiO2 renders the blue and only pale warm tones; the green, gold, and terracotta require the blue-band absorption of V2O5, and the low-contrast oxides turn grey at the warm end. The encapsulant governs the color, and the cell technology does not. Disorder does not confer angle independence: the colors hold for an observer moving under a fixed sun but drift by DeltaE00 of 5 to 18 over the day, terracotta least, and a facade lit from above and viewed from below shifts every color towards the blue. Oxide, layer number, and facade geometry must therefore be designed together.

Engineering Correlated Transparent Conductors via Metal-Insulator Transition Proximity

Amit Cohen, Nofar Tuval, Maria Baskin, Tien-Lin Lee, Tom Tiwald, Scott A. Chambers

Transparent conductors are key functional materials in modern optoelectronic devices. Their performance is governed by an inherent trade-off between optical transparency and electrical conductivity. Correlated metallic oxides offer an attractive workaround to this compromise, where narrow partially-filled bands provide high conductivity, while the absence of visible-range transitions enables optical transparency. Despite this promise, transparency and conductivity remain coupled through carrier density and electronic coherence. Here, we use proximity to a metal-insulator transition (MIT) as a design principle for engineering this balance in correlated-oxide transparent conductors. Epitaxial Ti-substituted $SrVO_3$ films serve as a model platform where the optical window and metallicity are tuned simultaneously. We demonstrate and explain a systematic broadening of the optical window to regimes relevant for silicon photovoltaics. This gain is counterbalanced by carrier depletion and disorder, which drive the loss of coherent transport near the composition-driven MIT. A Haacke figure-of-merit analysis identifies the optimal composition range near the MIT, where the loss of metallic transport is accompanied by persistent near-Fermi-level spectral weight, indicating disorder-assisted localization together with correlation-driven spectral-weight redistribution. These findings establish proximity to an MIT as a general materials-design strategy, using electronic filling as a tuning knob for the transparency-conductivity balance in correlated-oxide transparent conductors.

Dual-Resonator Organic Microcavities for Triplet-Triplet Annihilation Upconversion

Robert H. Gordon, Rachel C. Kilbride, Michael Hutton, Jenny Clark, Robert D. J. Oliver

Solid-state triplet-triplet annihilation upconversion offers a compelling route to harvest sub-bandgap photons to increase the efficiency of photovoltaics, but low sensitizer doping required to suppress non-radiative aggregation quenching severely restricts light absorption. Here, we present a dual-resonator planar silver microcavity housing a thermally co-evaporated rubrene:palladium phthalocyanine (PdPC) active layer designed to simultaneously enhance triplet-sensitizer absorption and out-couple upconverted emission. By aligning a dispersive cavity mode with the PdPC absorption peak at $32^\circ$, we achieve a 12-fold enhancement in absorptance at 665 nm. This absorption boost, combined with directional outcoupling through a second cavity mode, outweighs cavity-induced reabsorption losses to yield a 22-fold peak and 4.5-fold integrated enhancement in detected upconversion emission. Overall, this work highlights the potential of microcavity integration to substantially enhance solid-state upconversion performance across low-intensity optoelectronic applications.

Tellurium trioxide as an n-type wide-gap semiconductor with ultrahigh work functions from first principles

Tianwei Wang, Teruya Nagafuji, Kota Hanzawa, Hidenori Hiramatsu, Fumiyasu Oba

By using first principles calculations, we show that \b{eta}-TeO3, with a wide band gap exceeding 3 eV, emerges as a promising n-type semiconductor exhibiting ultrahigh work functions of up to ~8 eV. Intentionally or unintentionally incorporated interstitial H, Li, Na, Cu, Mg, and Zn impurities and substitutional F impurities are identified as shallow donors, whereas O vacancies act as minor carrier-compensating defects that locally trap electrons at neighbouring Te sites. Collectively, the results highlight the potential of \b{eta}-TeO3 for hole injection/extraction/transport layers as well as transparent electrodes for applications such as light-emitting devices and photovoltaic cells, while providing useful guidance on carrier doping and surface tuning for these practical applications through elucidating the effects of the point-defect and surface configurations on the electronic properties.

Composition-controlled optical fingerprints and anisotropy of self-trapped excitons in double perovskites

Simone Grillo, Offir Zachs Maishlos, Liya Koren, Rotem Strassberg, Rachel Lifer, Edi Maizenberg

Double perovskites (DPs) offer a compositionally versatile platform for photocatalysis and optoelectronics, but their soft lattices favor strong exciton-phonon coupling and self-trapped excitons (STEs), which produce broad, red-shifted photoluminescence (PL). Here, we combine optical spectroscopy and ab initio calculations to study lead-free, Sb-doped Cs2BInCl6 nanocrystals with B-site alloying from Na to K. Alloying tunes the PL energy from about 2.48 to 2.70 eV, while absorption features near 3.8 eV remain nearly unchanged. Calculations reveal that Sb doping enables bright radiative recombination channels and delocalizes the excitonic wavefunction. We identify STEs responsible for the large PL red shift, associated with an upward shift of dopant-hybridized in-gap states. Na-to-K substitution selectively changes the STE lattice distortion, explaining why PL varies strongly with composition while absorption does not. Theory further predicts pronounced PL polarization anisotropy that reflects the STE wavefunction's spatial structure and depends on B-site composition. Time-resolved anisotropy measurements closely agree with these predictions, providing experimental fingerprints of the excitonic wavefunction and its evolution with alloying. Together, these results disentangle the roles of doping and alloying in lead-free DPs and establish polarization-resolved spectroscopy as a probe of STE wavefunctions.
